# Compiler notes

This document records observed behaviour of the bundled Microsoft Visual C++ 6.0 compilers (the c1 and c1xx front ends and the c2 back end) that the reconstruction depends on. The findings come from compiling variants of `src/`, comparing the objects with the original executable and its PDB, and tracing c2 while it compiles. They explain why the same source behaviour can compile to different bytes, and why the steering markers in `src/` (`RAG_BUDGET_FILL`, `RAG_BUDGET_SITES`, `RAG_ENTROPY_SHIFT`, `RAG_SYMBOL_SHIFT`, `RAG_SYMBOL_CHAIN` and the headers `src/rag_*.h`) exist.

Addresses such as `0x10731dd1` are virtual addresses in the bundled `c2.dll`. "Masked lines" counts the differing lines of an instruction diff that masks jump targets and frame offsets.

## Inline budget

Measured from `/Bk` IL files and compiles of `src/`.

- The front end marks a function an automatic inline candidate (IL flag `0x40`) only below a size limit: bodies of IL size 160, 162 and 171 had the flag, 175, 183 and 187 did not. The loop form (`while`, `do`, `goto`) does not change it.
- The back end gives each compiled function a budget of twice its own IL size, with a floor of 1000. A callee of size 40 or less expands free; a larger one is charged its size and is called when the remaining budget is smaller.
- A nested candidate receives the caller's remaining budget divided by the number of the caller's candidate sites not yet passed, free sites included. An extra call of a small inline function (an accessor, the empty `Trace`) therefore changes which nested callees expand, and so does source that compiles to nothing but enlarges the function.
- For each procedure, the walk gives the own size and the site count that reproduce the original's decisions. Prefer the natural cause (an inline accessor the class already declares, as in `CGameMode::ProcessNameBalloon`). Where no evidence shows the missing source, `RAG_BUDGET_FILL` (size) and `RAG_BUDGET_SITES` (sites) stand in for it.
- When no single input reproduces the original, read the walk itself: each row gives a site's size, the remaining budget, the sites left and the share handed to its nested callees. A nested callee over size 40 expands when the share covers it (`vector::insert(it, x)` is 64, `basic_string::_Tidy` 152), so count the fillers each side of a site needs. Fillers before a site lower its share, and every filler raises the function's own size and so the budget. `UINameBalloonText::SetNameText` needs four sites before one `push_back` and three after the last.
- Fillers only enlarge a function. When the walk needs a smaller own size, the source is spelled larger than the original was. Measured on `SelectClientInfo2`:
  - `x = f(); if (x)` costs 3 more than `if (x = f())`;
  - `n = g(); v.push_back(n);` costs 5 more than `v.push_back(g())`;
  - braces around a single statement cost 1 to 2.

  The original's line records show which statements shared a line. Explicit-length calls written to dodge an inline decision (`s.compare(0, 6, "local", 5)` in `SetOption`) are wrong; the natural `s == "local"` with a filler matches.
- The back end also keeps a running total (c2 global `0x1079f234`), starting at the function's own size and adding every expanded callee, free ones included, plus each nested walk's used budget; past 35000 nothing more expands. It matters for functions whose own size is near 35000. `CGameMode::SendMsg` (own size 28783) matches with one dead `name.cName.c_str();`, `RAG_BUDGET_SITES(7)` after `name.Clear();` and `RAG_BUDGET_FILL(122)` (window 116 to 128), so that the total passes 35000 inside the seventh `map::operator[]` as in the original. `UIGuildMemberManageWnd::OnDraw` depends on this limit alone.
- A callee that destroys a class it takes by value (`void UIButton::SetText(std::string txt)`) is charged and then called (warning 4710), under `/GX`: c2 refuses it after the charge when an unwind state of its body names one of its parameters (0x10732442, 0x1077fb87). Its IL registers an unwind cleanup (0x5c) that is a destructor call on a parameter of its entry record. The call's operands carry a sequence token (0x44) after the object.
- A function that is itself inlined elsewhere is judged in every copy. `CTexMgr::GetTexture` is expanded inside `CRenderer::DrawBattleFieldIcon` and `DrawAgeRate`; a form that fixes the standalone body (calling `AddTexture`) breaks a caller. Check all of them after any change to such a function.
- A function can be made to spend budget without code: an effect-free call of an inline callee over size 40 (`m_texTable.begin();`, whose nested `_Tree::begin` is 41) is charged and then optimized away. Its own size counts against the inline-candidate limit, which for `GetTexture` lies between 174 (still a candidate) and 175 (not). Every self-assignment costs 5, `begin()` 14, and the `else` braces 2.

  `GetTexture` matches with the `NULL` branch written without `else`, `tex = tex; m_texTable.begin();` before the insert (own size 171, insert share 545 to 504), `RAG_BUDGET_FILL(10)` in `DrawBattleFieldIcon` (8 to 12 match) and `RAG_BUDGET_FILL(55)` in `DrawAgeRate` (52 to 58 match). The callers' windows follow from the walk: above the 1000 floor each filler step adds 4 own size and 8 budget, and `DrawAgeRate` needs the inlined `LoadTexture` share to reach 92 while the insert's share stays under 373. Compute such windows from the walk before scanning; a coarse scan steps over this one.
- Fewer top-level sites raise every later share. `CRenderer::AddSpriteIndex` needs a `CacheSurface&` and an index variable in place of repeated `surfaceIt->` and `info[y * w + x]` before `list::insert` stays a call while `list::erase` and `vector::erase` expand, as in the original.
- When an early nested callee needs a larger share and later ones a smaller share, the original has fewer sites between them and more after the later ones. `UIAuctionWnd::SendMsg` needs one `DRAG_INFO *dragInfo = gameMode->GetDragInfo();` in place of eight `gameMode->GetDragInfo()` accessor sites (the first `ITEM_INFO()` then expands `_Tidy`) and `RAG_BUDGET_SITES(3)` after `SetText(std::string(itemName))` (its `_Grow` and the `m_itemName` assign's final `_Eos` then stay calls). Neither alone gives the original's counts.
- `UIChatRoomWnd::SendMsg` matches with a filler for which no source evidence was found. The second `push_back` of the member switch's first case has a share of 7569 / 119 = 63 against the 64 of `insert(it, x)`, which the original expands there; 24 to 60 more units of own size, or one site fewer behind it, give the original's decisions.
  - `clear()` for `erase(begin(), end())` changes the code.
  - `empty()` for `size() != 0` changes nothing.
  - `return 0;` for `break;` in the menu-id switch adds 2 per case but keeps the code only with an even number of returns among the three cases that destroy a string (at most 10 in all).

  `RAG_BUDGET_FILL(10)` (6 to 15 match) stands in the code-free run behind the else branch's `if (myRole == 0)` block, original lines 4883 to 4889.
- `UIMessengerGroupWnd::SendMsg` matches with `RAG_BUDGET_FILL(28)`; 25 to 32 match (with the `default: break;` of "Tail merging, read from c2" in place). The original expands the second string's `_Tidy` in `FRIEND_INFO`'s destructor from `ID_FRIEND_INFO` on and keeps `ID_TALK`'s a call. The filler stands in the code-free run after `if (info.isValid)` in `ID_SENDMSG` (original lines 714 to 720).
- `CGameMode::PollNetworkStatus` matches with `RAG_BUDGET_FILL(7)` at the top (4 to 10 by the walk; 4, 6, 7 and 9 measured), `RAG_BUDGET_SITES(14)` after `Zc_Ack_Reqname(buf);` and `RAG_BUDGET_SITES(14)` after the default case's `ResetQueue();`, on top of the three case placements described under "Reading the compiler's decisions". The site fillers keep the nested `string::assign(const char*, size_t)` of `Zc_Ack_Reqname` and `Zc_Req_Join_Group` a call (share under 69) while `SetMemberVisibleState` inside `Zc_Guild_Member_Map_Change` still expands (share 58 or more, so at most about 16 fillers may follow it; 12 + 16 fails, 14 + 14 and 16 + 12 match).

  The original PDB declares `void CGameMode::Zc_Reassembly_Certify(const int)` last in the class and records no `p` locals in the function, so its 42 certify cases went through that inline member. The member form is not reproduced: with the whole body in the member (size 89, own size 6849) the budget is about 2600 short and the last case cannot expand, and no split measured gives the original's decisions. `src/` keeps the `{ p = {0}; ...; ReassemblyCertify(p); }` sites with the fillers.
- `UIWindowMgr::MakeWindow` matches with `RAG_BUDGET_FILL(255)` and `RAG_BUDGET_FILL(96)`; 93 to 100 match for the second, at the top of the function as well. They stand in the code-free run after `g_modeMgr.GetGameMode();` in the first case (original lines 3995 to 3999). The original expands 53 `AddWindow(wnd)` sites in full, expands `AddWindow` alone in `WID_SAYDIALOGWND` (its `push_back` is a call) and calls `AddWindow` at the 28 sites after it. A `m_children.push_back(wnd)` written there to get that call changes the code of every other site ("Alias classes of indirections, read from c2").
- A site marker can stand for another spelling of a container access. `(*v.rbegin())` is two free sites (`rbegin`, the reverse iterator's `operator*`) where `v.back()` is one, and a use inside the `max` macro counts twice. In the `SetNameText` family in `UIControl.cpp`, `UINameBalloonText::SetNameText(const char*)` and `UIVerticalNameBalloonText::SetNameText(const NamePair&, bool)` match with no marker once the last-element reads use `rbegin()` (the second: one of the two reads in the first-name statement and the read after the last `push_back`; every other choice of the six reads fails); the horizontal `NamePair` overload in the same form needs one site after its last `push_back` instead of seven. The developers use `*x.rbegin()` elsewhere (`CreateUnusedCacheSurfaces`, `3dGround.cpp`, `Language.cpp`). The constraints that decide it, read from the walk:
  - the fourth `push_back`'s share must stay under 64 (`insert` called);
  - the fifth string constructor's share must lie between 89 and 151 (`assign(ptr, n)` expanded, `_Tidy` called);
  - the fifth temporary's destructor needs 152 or more (`_Tidy` expanded);
  - callees of size 40 or less take a site but no budget.
- The original's own call counts are the ground truth for inline decisions, not a predicted walk: count the calls per callee in the original procedure and in ours and close the differences first. (A predicted walk had `Zc_Congratulation` called inside `CGameMode::PollNetworkStatus`; the original expands it.)
- Each destructor site of a local is a candidate site. An early `return` inside the scope of a destructible local adds one, so `if (!ok) return;` and a single exit through `if (ok) { ... }` give later nested callees different shares (`CGameMode::Zc_Enter_Room`: the single exit makes `ChatRoomInfo()`'s first string constructor expand as in the original).
- `it++` on a class iterator is a charged site where `++it` is not, with the same final code when the copy is unused (`Zc_Enter_Room`).
- An inlined callee in a destructor that several exits of one scope share (a `break` out of the scope and its end) is expanded per destructor site, and tail merging then folds the copies across scopes. The remaining code shows fewer inlined copies than the walk expanded, and their positions (which copy keeps the shared tail) follow the merge order, not the walk. `UIMessengerGroupWnd::SendMsg` shows two `_Tidy(true)` copies on both sides from different per-site decisions. A constant held in a register only in the original (`or esi,-1` there, an immediate in ours) tells a `break` form from an `if` form: the break's destructor copy adds one more weighed use of the state constant, even after merging removes the copy.
- Code inlined into a function carries the line of the call site, not the callee's lines, so the line table cannot tell an inline helper from a macro or handwritten block.

## Codegen entropy

Some functions' register and stack choices depend on the number of declarations the unit has seen, modulo 64. Compiling a procedure under each of the 64 shifts shows whether it is sensitive and which shifts make it equal. `RAG_ENTROPY_SHIFT` pairs (`src/rag_entropy_pad.h`) apply a shift before the function and restore the count after it. An inline function defined in a header counts for every unit that includes it.

Adding a filler shifts the count for everything the unit compiles after it: the `#include` of a filler header counts 1 where it stands, and each `RAG_BUDGET_FILL` local counts 1 for later functions. After adding one, measure the whole unit again. If a later function changes, wrap that function alone in a `RAG_ENTROPY_SHIFT` pair whose two shifts and include sum to 64 (`UIPetTamingDeceiveWnd::OnDraw`).

A declaration added to a shared header shifts the count of every unit that includes it; a struct added to `src/packet.h` changes procedures in three units, padded and unpadded. After header edits, measure the whole project, scan each changed procedure again and retune its pair. A pair must sum to 64 (the include of `rag_entropy_pad.h` counts 1, so the first pair in a unit sums to 63); otherwise every later function in the unit carries the shift as well.

Try the original's order before a pad. The count a function sees depends on what the unit defines before it, so a function or a global defined in a different place than the original's shifts it. Four functions match with no pad once their unit is in the original's order: `UIItemPurchaseWnd::SendMsg` and `UIItemSellWnd::SendMsg` (function order), `CBitmapRes::SetAlphaWithBMP` (the three static instances moved ahead of the default constructor) and `CSession::InitTable`. Removing the pair of `UIPetTamingDeceiveWnd::OnDraw` or of `CreateDependsDllMsg` changes only the padded function.

Both pads stand for declarations missing at the head of their unit. Without its restore each unit keeps every other function, and `typedef int` lines after the unit's last leading include make the padded function match with no pad: 29 to 32 in `base/FindHack.cpp`, 38 to 49 in `UIFrameWnd2.cpp` without `resource/imfres.h` and `GuildInfo.h`. With those two includes, the unit's initialiser helpers are `$E99` and `$S100` as in the original (`$E93` and `$S94` without them), and, with the file-scope declarations still in the units (see below), `OnDraw` is one declaration short of its window (a pad of 5, 0 to 11 match, with the pad header's include counting 1). A second include of a guarded header counts nothing; the first include of a file counts 1. `FindHack.cpp` declares the nProtect key SDK's types itself; its pad stands for the rest of those headers.

A `const float` at file scope leaves its four bytes in the `.rdata` of every unit that sees it, used or not, which dates includes: the original `Sound.obj`, `PathFinder.obj` and `LanguageKeyProcess.obj` hold `pi`, so `Sound.cpp` includes `Prim.h`, `Attr.h` includes it, and `LanguageKeyProcess.cpp` includes it (marked as inferred). `common.h` holds a block of globals that the original's line numbers need (`Trace` at 69, `CharPrtLess::operator()` at 106), with `compress` and `uncompress` declared `dllimport` because `GPak.cpp`, `UIGuildWnd.cpp` and `Emblem.cpp` call them through the import pointer.

Moving file-scope declarations from units into headers changes the bytes of four count-sensitive functions and of nothing else. Two take a pair: `UIPetTamingDeceiveWnd::OnDraw` 42 and 21, and `CreateDependsDllMsg` 42 and 21. Two match with an include instead:

- `UIItemPurchaseWnd::SendMsg` (`UIItemWnd.cpp`) with `base/Util.h` straight behind `MsgStrings.h`. Behind `skin.h`, or ahead of `device/Connection.h`, it does not match: the place of an include counts, not only the number of declarations.

The keys c2 sorts by are the same for `OnDraw` with and without its pad (339 comparisons), so this entropy is not in them. `UIFrameWnd2.cpp` with other include blocks, with and without `base/Util.h` and `3dlib/DrawUtil.h`, keeps every other function and leaves `OnDraw` unmatched. ## Order of code sections in an object

The linker places an object's `.text` sections in section-table order, so the address order of the original's contributions is the order in which its compiler emitted them. Sort the original's contributions of one source by address and compare that with the object's section order: a section outside the longest increasing run is misplaced. `.text$x` sections sort after `.text` and are a separate sequence. The first line record of each original procedure in the PDB gives the original file and line.

- An ordinary function is emitted where the unit defines it. c2 inlines a function defined later in the unit (`CHash::SetString` into the constructors before it), so moving a definition to the original's place leaves the bytes of every function unchanged. A declaration that sat between two functions has to stay ahead of its first user.
- A global with a dynamic initialiser emits its `_$E` functions where the global is defined, followed by the inline functions the initialiser needs. The resource classes define their static instance ahead of the default constructor (`_$E`, `ctor(Exemplar, ...)`, `Clone`, the destructors, then the unit's own functions).
- A global defined between two functions also fixes the place of its storage among the function-level statics in `.data` and `.bss`, so it moves with the function that follows it (`nextCacheUnloadTime` ahead of `CGameMode::OnUpdate`).
- Inline functions are emitted after the function that first needs them, in the order the unit defined them, so their order is the order the headers were first included (`GameMode2.cpp` includes `GameMode.h` first: `packet.h`, `UISys.h`, then `File.h`, `ItemInfo.h`, `SkillInfo.h`, `Session.h`).
- Members of class templates are emitted in the order the classes were instantiated, which for members of a class is their declaration order (`CGameMode`'s maps and lists; `C3dPosAnim`, `C3dRotAnim`, `C3dScaleAnim` in `Anim.h`).
- A function the original took from a library template is an instantiation, not a definition of the unit: `operator<(pair<float, RPFace*>)` of `Renderer.cpp` is `<utility>`'s, at `utility:39` in the line records.
- Members of class templates are emitted in rounds. The first round holds the members the unit's own text asks for: function bodies, unused static functions included, and the implicit calls of constructors and destructors. Each later round holds what the bodies of the round before asked for. Within a round the members stand grouped by class, and the classes in the order in which the unit first asked for a member of each, a member that leaves no code (an inlined constructor) included; so the headers whose class definitions and inline bodies ask first decide it.

  Measured on `UIWindowMgr.cpp`: `m_chatWndHistory.erase(begin, end)` written in the constructor, in the destructor, at line 5450 or in an unused static function at the top of the file puts `list<StringAndColor>::erase(iterator, iterator)` right behind `~list<StringAndColor>` each time; without it the member comes a round later, asked for by the destructor's body. So spellings that compile to the same bytes still change the order:
  - `it++` on a list iterator references `iterator::operator++(int)` directly, while `++it` leaves it to `erase(first, last)` (`CWorld::MakeSkill`, `UIItemIdentifyWnd::OnDraw`, `UIGuildMemberManageWnd::GetCurItem`; the order shows that one loop of the unit uses `it++`, not which one);
  - a typedef naming `vector<PakPack>::iterator` in `GPak.h` instantiates `vector<PakPack>` ahead of the classes below it.
- The order of two classes in a header and of two inline members in a class is the order of their instantiations and bodies (`CPathInfo` ahead of `CPriorityQueue`, `Push` ahead of `Pop` in `PathFinder.h`); the order of two includes decides it between headers (`GPak.h` ahead of `File.h` in `File.cpp`).
- A function the unit's text defines asks for the implicit functions its body needs where it stands, and an explicit specialization of a library template is such a function. `std::copy_backward<CMotion*>` written as a specialization in `Action.cpp` and defined at the top emits `CMotion::operator=` ahead of the unit's own functions; declared at the top and defined at the end it still leaves eight sections out of place. The library template gives the original's order ("Declaration count in the keys of calls").
- The original project compiled with automatic precompiled headers through `common.h` (`/YX"common.h"`, a Visual C++ 6 project setting). An inline function that the precompiled part defines is emitted after the unit's own inline functions and among the template members, not in definition order: `Trace` last in `Device.cpp`, `CInputMode::ResetMode` in `telexInputMode.cpp`, `strstreambuf`'s constructor after `CTokenizer::operator[]` in `Action.cpp`. Defining `CInputMode::ResetMode` outside its class does not move it.

  c1 writes such a function at the first function boundary after the unit has produced as much function code as stood ahead of that function in the compile that made the PCH. What counts is intermediate code of function bodies, used or not: statements of an unused `static` function, string assignments, one long expression and unused one-line inline functions all move the four functions of `Action.cpp` the same way, one inline one-liner counting about 2.5 assignment statements. Blank lines, typedefs, function declarations, class definitions and extern variables move them by one place at most. Inline functions added to `common.h` ahead of `<string>` delay the PCH functions; the same functions after `<strstream>` or at the end of `common.h` change nothing. So the inline functions of the unit's own headers (`Util.h`, `Prim.h`) decide where they land.

  Whether a unit made the header or read one shows in its object. Every section's bytes are the same in every mode; the table counts misplaced `.text` sections:

  | Unit | no PCH | makes it (`/YX` or `/Yc`) | reads a `/YX`-made one | reads a `/Yc`-made one (`/Yu`) |

  | `resource/Action.cpp` | 4 | 0 | 3 | 0, static-local guards move in `.bss` |

  | `base/Util.cpp` | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
  | `telexInputMode.cpp` (no `common.h`: `/YX` precompiles all its leading headers) | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

  The other 109 sources that include `common.h` give the same object in the first three modes. `/Yu` moves file-scope statics and static-local guards in `.bss` in 33 units (348 procedures no longer match with it), so no unit read a `/Yc`-made header: the one `Common.cpp` makes served no other unit.

  A build simulated in module order, which is the compile order, with one shared `.pch` file has `xml.cpp` (module 70) make the header and `Util.cpp` (71) read it, as the object needs. But it lets `Action.cpp` (132) read the header `Attr.cpp` (131) made, and no unit from 21 to 32 replaces the header `GronModePacket.cpp` (20) made, so `telexInputMode.cpp` (29) comes out as without one. In reverse order `Action.cpp` and `telexInputMode.cpp` make their own and `Util.cpp` is wrong. The `Makefile` therefore gives the four units their mode, with the object as evidence: one `/YX"common.h"` compile in an empty stage for `Action.cpp`, `Device.cpp` and `telexInputMode.cpp`, and two such compiles, the second kept, for `Util.cpp`.

  `/YX` with no header name (a PCH of every leading include) misplaces sections in 59 of the 112 units. `/Yu` also skips what stands ahead of the include of `common.h`; `D3dutil.cpp`'s `#define OUTPUTSTR` stands behind its includes for that reason.
- The sections of a unit's initialised data appear in the object in the reverse order of the definitions that first need them, and the linker keeps the object's order within `.data`. In `ItemInfo.cpp`, `IsNumberString`'s static local `number[256]` is the unit's only initialised datum besides four `static const char* const` strings:
  - with the strings defined ahead of `IsNumberString`, the object has `.data` (the table), `.rdata` (`pi` and the four pointers) and then the string literals `sorry`, the Korean one and `Unknown Item` (last defined first);
  - with the strings defined behind it, just ahead of `ITEM_INFO::InitItemInfoTables`, it has `.rdata`, the three literals and then the table, which is the original's image order.

  Dropping `static`, `extern const` and a place straight ahead of `IsNumberString` change nothing; a non-const pointer moves the pointers into `.data`.
- The linker loads a library member when it first meets an undefined reference to it, so the order of two CRT members follows the names the objects use: `strlwr` in `CGameMode::Zc_Broadcast` resolves through OLDNAMES after `strtok`; `_strlwr` would load `strlwr.obj` first.

## Symbol-number shift

c2 orders commutative operands by a hash that contains the numbers of the function's symbols and temporaries (see the sort keys under "Reading the compiler's decisions"). Temporaries are numbered after the source symbols, so one more symbol in the function shifts every temporary by one and can flip operand orders anywhere in it.

`RAG_SYMBOL_SHIFT(n)` (`src/rag_symbol_shift.h`) adds n symbols and no code: n blocks of one local and one dead store. It is the symbol count that matters, not size:

- `RAG_SYMBOL_SHIFT(3)` makes `C3dGround15::Render` match where `RAG_BUDGET_FILL(3)` (one symbol, same size) does not;
- `RAG_SYMBOL_SHIFT(1)` makes `C3dModelRes::Load` match (9 and 13 also match), and the position inside the function does not matter there;
- `RAG_SYMBOL_SHIFT(8)` makes `CEffectPrim::RenderGroundSample` match.

Use it after natural forms fail on a residue that is only operand order; past about 14 blocks the added own size starts to change inlining. The locals also count for the codegen entropy of later functions, so measure the unit again. A function that holds one is steered, and so are callers that inline it.

### Self-assignments

Where only the temporaries after some point are off (the operand-order pairs before it already match), a symbol shift moves them all. A self-assignment `E = E;` instead adds temporaries where it stands (its loads, address arithmetic and store) and leaves no code. The late temporaries numbered before the first wrong pair show where it must stand. It also adds own size, so retune any filler that the function holds. `m_viewChar[slot].x = m_viewChar[slot].x;` before the name lookups, with the loop's filler cut from 10 to 4 (2 to 5 match), makes `UISelCharForUServerWnd::OnCreate` match. It declares nothing, so later functions keep their entropy count. The project counts any statement whose two sides are the same tokens as a symbol shift.

### Chunks of 32

A shift can also work through the 32-symbol chunks alone: symbols fill chunks of 32 and the late temporaries are numbered from the next chunk boundary, so in a function with 25 symbols eight more move every late temporary by 0x20 and seven move none (measured on `std::copy_backward<CMotion*>`, which needs no shift once its declaration count is right; see "Declaration count in the keys of calls"). A function that other functions inline charges its own size to their budgets: eight `RAG_SYMBOL_SHIFT` blocks there change the inlining in `vector<CMotion>::insert` from five blocks on, while `RAG_SYMBOL_CHAIN(8)` (eight locals, one chained store) shifts the same and leaves `insert` unchanged. A template instance takes a shift only through an explicit specialization with the template's body (same decorated name, same code), which also moves the unit's sections ("Order of code sections in an object"). Unreferenced locals and empty blocks add no symbols.

### Macros against inline functions

A function-like macro in place of an inline function also changes the numbering: an inline expansion gives its parameters symbols and temporaries, a macro gives none. `C3dNode::UpdateVertexColor` (`src/3dActor.cpp`) with `fmin` / `fmax` as macros has 125 masked lines in four sum and product orders. The inline functions that `src/3dlib/Prim.cpp` and `src/3dGround.cpp` define (`inline float fmax(float a, float b) { return a > b ? a : b; }`) cut it to 32 lines with no steering; `RAG_SYMBOL_CHAIN(2)` makes it match. Other bodies (a result local, an assigned parameter, `if` forms, reference parameters) are worse.

Define such helpers just before their first user: two inline definitions at the top of the unit shift the entropy count of every later function and change `C3dNode::Render`. A shift block before a static local changes the static's decorated name (`?m_faceLight@?1??...` becomes `?2??`), so its `.bss` offset moves; put the chain after the statics. When a sum's order is the only difference, the operand keys and the product rule (product key = higher + 2 * lower + 0x2a) say which numbers each order needs. Measure that before sweeping.

### Dereference against symbol in `_Eos` stores

For the inlined `basic_string::_Eos` stores (`mov byte ptr [eax+ecx], 0` against `[ecx+eax]`), the deciding comparison is between a dereference (op 0x14c) and a kind-4 symbol (op 0x14a). The dereference sorts first iff `0x10007 + 0x4000 * (base temporary mod 4)` exceeds `0x10000 + 0x20 * symbol number` (measured on all pairs of both character-select `OnCreate`s):

- below symbol 0x400 the dereference goes first for temporaries 2 and 3 mod 4;
- from 0x400 to 0x5ff only for 3.

So a site flips either when its temporary moves or when its symbol crosses 0x400. A temporary compared as an operand (op 0x14a with a temporary N) has key `0x10000 + 0x40 * (N mod 0x400)`, so a dereference goes before it iff `N mod 0x400 <= 0x100 * (base mod 4)`. `C3dNode::Render`'s `m_destVertCol` address matches this way: `m_numTexture = m_numTexture; m_opacity = m_opacity;` before the loop add 6 temporaries ahead of the base.

`UIPartySettingWnd::OnCreate`'s two `m_nYOffset + c + i * 20` sites share one base `b` and need opposite turns: the first needs its temporary `n1 mod 0x400 <= 0x100 * (b mod 4)`, the second `n2 mod 0x400 > 0x100 * (b mod 4)`, with `n2 - n1` about 0x77. Any statement at the top jumps `b` from 0x4e9 to about 0x500 and `n1` from 0x7cd to 0x800 or 0x801. With `b mod 4 = 1`, `n2` must pass 0x900, and once inlining is held equal each further statement adds only 1 to it. So the solution is `b mod 4 = 0` with `n1 = 0x800` exactly:

- `m_nYOffset = m_nYOffset;` first (it becomes `b` itself, 0x500);
- `m_x = m_x; m_h = m_h;` before the second block;
- `m_radioItemDiv[i]->m_isEnabled = m_radioItemDiv[i]->m_isEnabled;` in the first loop after site 1;
- the function's filler cut from 200 to 150 (130 to 180 match).

A statement after a site can still move that site's temporary, because the numbering does not follow source order, and a filler that changes inlining moves every number by 0x20 or more: measure the temporaries with the filler already inside its window.

A self-assignment first in the body is worth sweeping wherever an operand order is the only difference: `m_x = m_x;` makes `UIGuildInfoManageWnd::SendMsg` match and `m_w = m_w;` makes `UICommandListWnd::OnDraw` match, which symbol shifts cannot, since they renumber both temporaries of a pair alike. What a statement adds depends on the statement and on its place: in `UISelectCharWnd::OnCreate`'s loop, a self-assignment of a member not otherwise used there adds 3 temporaries, `m_curPage` 2, and globals, locals and `charInfo` fields 0. Each also adds own size and so moves inlining; the budget window there allows about one extra statement. `RAG_SYMBOL_SHIFT(1)` there moves the inline symbols but not the late temporaries, and lifts one site's symbol from 0x3bf to 0x440. With the `m_viewChar[slot].x` self-assignment (+1 temporary at every site) the function matches, with the filler cut from 10 to 2 (1 to 4 match).

A late temporary used as a dereference base moves only with temporaries numbered before it, and source order does not decide which those are. In `UIAuctionWnd::SendMsg`, `m_item.m_num = m_item.m_num;` before `SetText(std::string(itemName))` moves the `_Eos` base from 0x7d5 to 0x7d6, a second self-assignment of `m_itemIndex` gets its temporary after the base, and one of `m_itemType` or `m_refiningLevel` gives 0x7d7, which matches. Measure the late temporaries of each pair rather than reasoning from position.

### Symbol pools

c2 takes symbols from several pools, each in chunks of 32 (allocator `0x10701411`, chunk `0x10704fe1`), and numbers a chunk's 32 symbols from one counter when the chunk is allocated. A symbol's number is its chunk's base plus its slot, so when a pool runs out of its chunk a few allocations earlier or later, its later symbols jump by whatever the counter has reached meanwhile.

Inline parameter temporaries come from a pool with a free list (`[0x1079bc70]`, free list `[0x1079bc84]`). A parameter the callee never assigns is freed after the expansion and reused; an assigned one keeps its temporary. `UIMailListWnd::OnCreate` has four `SetTextLimit` expansions, so the clamp keeps four more temporaries. That pool's chunk then runs out in an earlier pass. The `_Eos` index of the `inputzeny` string becomes 0x645 (key 0x1c8a0) instead of a fresh 0x8e1 (key 0x11c21), which flips `[eax + ecx]`.

A self-assignment can also move only the later chunk. In `UIMakeCharWnd::SendMsg`, four to six `m_x = m_x;` in front of the `ID_AGI` case add 3 to the sum's temporaries, and the seventh adds 0x20 more to every later temporary. Seven placed after the case (first in `ID_VIT`) give only the 0x20, which moves the sum's `Luk` temporary to 0x3fe and `Agi` to 0x402, so `Luk` sorts first as in the original, and the five `_Eos` sites match too. Their own size then inlines one more `max_size()`, so the function's filler is cut from 16 to 9 (4 to 14 match).

The late temporaries (pool 74, allocator caller `0x10708beb`) are numbered consecutively from one base, the counter value when their first chunk is allocated, so that base moves by 0x20 with every chunk the earlier pools (pool 5c, caller `0x10701f0f`, which continues the symbol numbers; pool 70) take before it. Tracing the allocations gives the chunks. `Tetris::DrawAreaBlocks` with pool 5c three numbers into chunk 0x3c0 has its late base at 0x3e0, which orders `AfterFrame` (late 0x418) after `iy * 30` (late 0x4c7). Deleting almost any statement drops the base to 0x3c0 or lower, and the natural `short area = Area[ix][iy];` with the four `Area[ix][iy] ==` tests reading `area` does the same with identical code: base 0x3c0, `AfterFrame` 0x3f8 sorts first, byte-identical (`int area` changes the code). Spellings of the sum itself never change this, since the sum only reads the numbers. Symbol shifts (1 to 23), a self-assignment at each of the 122 statements, entropy pads and filler moves leave it unchanged. Extra kept temporaries do restore it at 27 to 29 (-4 mod 32).

Single-parameter calls cost too much inline budget past about 23, but one call of an inline function that assigns all 28 of its parameters, next to the function's filler, has the same effect. That function is `rag_symbol_shift(int a0, ..., int a27)`, defined in the unit just before the function; its name marks it as a symbol shift, and its definition does not move any later function's entropy count there. ## Reading the compiler's decisions

Tracing the bundled `c2.dll` while it compiles one function (named by its decorated name; `strings` on the object finds it) shows why two instruction-equivalent compiles differ, which spelling experiments alone do not.

- c2 colours register candidates in a fixed order: by descending `key`, and equal keys by `tie`, larger first. `tie` is a position in the function, in statement-sized units, tied to the candidate's definitions and not to its first reference. On `SeedEncrypt` a statement before the loop raises the tie of both the counter (defined before the loop) and the loop's pointer temporary, a statement in the loop body raises only the temporary's, and a redefinition of the counter after the loop moves its tie past the temporary's and swaps the two registers.

  The writer is `0x1071b9bf`: a walk over the tuples at register allocation stores a running position into candidate `+0x40` at each symbol leaf (op `0x149`) that defines it, so the tie is the position of the candidate's last definition in tuple order, not in final code order. On `SeedEncrypt` the counter's last definition is its loop-exit value (`nDone = count * 16`, position 9), which c2 lists before the pointer's preheader load (0x11) and loop increment (0x14), although the scheduler later places it after the load. Loop forms that move it (`while (1)` with `break`, `if` around `do`/`while`, `nLength - 16 >= 0`) also change the loop transform.

  A swapped pair of registers is usually two candidates with equal keys: `SeedEncrypt` in `PacketProtect.cpp` has its byte counter and the loop's pointer both at key 44, and one more reference to the counter (key 48) gives the original's registers. Renaming a local, moving its declaration and changing its integer type leave key and tie unchanged.
- A temporary without a coloured register takes `eax`, `ecx`, `edx` in rotation, skipping a busy one, in the order of the instruction list at that point, which follows the source order of the blocks and not the final layout. A run of instructions whose scratch registers are rotated by one against the original therefore means a block stands at another place in the original source. In `cSecureGamePack::InitSecureGamePack`, a `return 1;` inside `case 1` places that block after the error report; the original's line records give the case's exit the function's last line, so it ends in `break` and the report is guarded by `if (rc != 1)`, which the compiler threads away. Look for exits whose line number is the last line of the function.
- A load that the original puts in eax without a rotation step can be an inlined callee's `this` that c2 kept as a symbol: the symbol is coloured and takes no scratch turn. When copy propagation replaces it by the load temporary, the temporary takes a turn. The late tuples show which happened: `sK = load; [sK+off] = ...` or `[tN+off] = ...`.

  For `UIEditCtrl::SetTextLimit`, a `new` of a class with a constructor later in the function keeps the symbol, whatever `SetTextLimit` holds. The three `OnCreate`s without one (`UIExchangeWnd`, `UIChangeNameWnd`, `UIMakeCharWnd`) need the original's body, which clamps its argument: `if (maxchar < 0) maxchar = 0; m_maxchar = maxchar;` (`src/window/UISys.h`). Every call passes a constant, so the clamp leaves no code; what matters is that the parameter is assigned. `<= 0`, an added upper clamp, or the clamp through a local also match. A conditional expression, an early return or assigning the member first leave the three unmatched, or change the inlining of other callers. One assigned-parameter temporary from an unrelated inline call does not make them match. The assigned parameter also keeps one temporary per expansion, which can renumber later symbols ("Symbol-number shift": `UIMailListWnd::OnCreate`).
- A load that sits in a coloured register in the original where ours takes a scratch turn can also come from another library accessor. In `CRenderer::AddSpriteIndex`, in the branch that reuses a pooled texture, the original's first `_Head` load is in eax and the rotation starts one step later than with `back()`. `surface.tex = *m_unusedCacheSurfaces.rbegin();` gives exactly that (the reverse iterator's copy of `current` stays a symbol); `back()`, `*(--end())`, a named iterator and `erase(--end())` do not (44 masked lines with `back()`, 10 with `rbegin()`, all of them the null EH state described under "Reading the original before searching"). When the registers of an inlined container call are rotated, try the container's other spellings of the same access before steering.
- Scratch turns that the original takes with no code to show for them belong to a tail that c2 merged away. When two blocks end in the same statements, the tail merge keeps the later copy with its line numbers, and the earlier copy still took its turns at its source position. The signs are a rotation that is off by the tail's turns from one block on, a code-free run in the original's line table at the end of the earlier block, and the kept statements following the later block's last statement with no line left for a closing brace.

  `UIExchangeWnd::OnDraw`: the `if` block and the `else` block each end in the two `TextOutA(..., name, ...)` calls (one turn each); lines 8386 to 8389 carry no code and the pair has lines 8393 and 8394, directly after the `else` block's `sprintf` at 8392. With the pair written once after the `if`/`else`, 85 masked lines differ and a filler would stand in the code-free run; the duplicated pair matches with no steering. Count the turns the original is ahead and look for following statements that take that many.
- Scratch turns with no code can also belong to a whole case whose inlined body c2 merged into an identical copy elsewhere. The copy still takes its turns where its `case` stands in the source, and identical bodies merge only when their scratch registers agree, so the jump table shows which copies met in the same rotation: values that share a target are either labels of one case or separate cases that merged.

  `CGameMode::PollNetworkStatus`: the original's table groups {771, 772, 773} and {774, 775} (the `Zc_Notify_Npc_Standentry__mN` cases, six inline members with one body); with `case 772: case 773:` and `case 765: case 767:` as shared labels and 771 elsewhere, the table groups {771, 774, 775} and {772, 773}. The original has each value as its own case: 771 between `Zc_Notify_Vanish` and `Sc_Notify_Ban`, 772 before `Zc_Item_Entry`, 765 (a `Zc_Notify_Moveentry5__mN` copy) before `Zc_Broadcast`. With the three in place the table groups and the block order equal the original's, and the certify sites 821 and 826 to 828 take the original's registers. The original's line table agrees: a hidden case shows as source lines without records (five per case here).

  Rotation differences are local: a temporary that must skip a register lands on the same one from two rotation states, so a shift ends at the next such spot and each shifted run needs its own cause.
- An inline accessor's result takes part in the rotation where the member itself does not. In `CGameMode::SetMemberVisibleState`, `GetWorld()->GetGameActorByAID(AID)` against `m_world->GetGameActorByAID(AID)` gives the same standalone bytes, but inside `PollNetworkStatus` (inlined through `Zc_Guild_Member_Map_Change`) the pushed `AID` takes `edx` with the accessor and `ecx` without, which moves certify site 854 by one register. Check the accessor spelling of every callee that a large function inlines.
- c2 orders commutative operands by comparing keys. The key is the Sethi-Ullman number, the node count and a hash of the operand; for a symbol the hash is its number shifted left 5 bits, for a temporary 6 bits. Numbers are per function, in creation order. `[edx+edi]` against `[edi+edx]` (`C3dNode::Render`) and the alternating `mov byte ptr [eax+ecx], 0` sites of `std::string::_Eos` (`UISelectCharWnd::OnCreate`) are decided here. Index spellings, pointer forms, unused and dead locals, and entropy pads do not change the order; a comparison between a temporary and a memory operand was 80 temporaries from flipping.
- Some storage is ordered by a hash of the declared names: the frame slots of exception-handling functions and `.bss` statics.
- Tail merging (cross-jumping) is described under "Tail merging, read from c2" below.

Three causes were measured and excluded for the functions above:

- the order of function definitions in the file (18 sources in the original's address order leave every procedure unchanged);
- the optimisation flags: `/Ot`, `/Os`, `/O1`, `/O2`, `/G5`, `/G6` on `PacketProtect.cpp`, and 26 combinations of `/O1`, `/O2`, `/Ox`, `/Og`, `/Oi`, `/Ot`, `/Os`, `/Oy-`, `/Ob2`, `/Oa`, `/Ow` and `/G3` to `/G6` on `PacketProtect.cpp`, `SafeAPI.cpp` and `NPGameLib.cpp`: none comes closer than the configured flags on all three, and `/G6` alone takes `SeedEncrypt` from 66 differing bytes to 63 and worsens the other two;
- the compiler build (Enterprise RTM and the 8047 beta give the same `SeedEncrypt`).

Symbol shifts do not reach them either: 1 to 64 blocks leave `loadDLL` at 1 byte or worse (11 from 27 blocks on), 1 to 32 leave `SeedEncrypt` at 66; nor do 39 loop and tail forms of `SeedEncrypt` (a separate block pointer, an explicit block count, an early return). `SeedEncrypt` and `loadDLL` match with natural source forms ("Source forms that changed codegen"); the measurements above are what does not reach them.

### `CGameGuard::PreInitNPGameMonI`

Three source forms make it match, none of them steering.

- The name copy of the inlined `CLog::Open` stands in each arm of the name test: `strcpy(slash ? (char*)slash + 1 : path, fileName);` in the arm that asks for the module's directory and `strcpy(path, fileName);` in the other, with `CreateFileA` behind the test. One `strcpy(name, fileName);` behind the test, with a `name` pointer set in the arms, gives the same code for `Open` (the tail merge joins the copies), but written once the copy shares its block with `CreateFileA`, and that block has no free register (`rep movs` and a call; "The split of candidates left without a register" below), joins no region and gets a marker on both sides. ## Tail merging, read from c2

The branch pass (0x10723474) hands each label to the driver (0x10729090), which pairs the unconditional jumps in the label's reference list, in instruction-list order, and calls the merge routine (0x1073f41d) with A later in the list than B. The routine walks both jumps' predecessors backwards while they are equal. Each instruction has an opcode (dword +4) and an instruction kind (byte +8; 0x0c is a compare, and 0x11 is discussed below). The pair merges, A's equal tail becoming a jump into B's:

- always, when the global at 0x107ac0b4 is 0 (not the case under the project's flags);
- when A's first unequal instruction has kind 0x0f, or is a kind 0x11 node whose word +0x20 is 0 and whose opcode is not 0x18b (free);
- through the swap path, when B's first unequal instruction is of that kind (B is redirected into A instead; free);
- when the walk passed such a node or a kind-0x13 node, merging at that point;
- or when the byte size of the equal tail plus the larger of the two jumps' words +0x12 is more than 20.

A merge by size stores the total in B's word, so a jump that already received one merge takes later ones easily. The driver resets the words to 0 for every label. The sizes are those of the instructions as encoded at merge time, before frame offsets and the late peephole: an exception state store counts as a 7-byte dword move and a frame operand as disp32. Two `return 0;` blocks that each destroy one string (`push 1`, `lea ecx,[obj]`, state store, `call`) therefore total exactly 20 and stay apart.

In `UIChatRoomWnd::SendMsg` the original has one such block, reached by a `je` from the first test: `if (it != m_memberList.end()) { int memberRole = m_memberList[memberName]; if (memberRole == -1) return 0; } else return 0;`. The `else` block follows the then-block's jump, so it merges for free. `if (it == end || m[name] == -1) return 0;` moves its one block to the end of the scope instead. Two separate `if`s keep two blocks.

Node kinds seen in the walk: labels are kind 0x1a (opcode 0x1ae) and the walk steps over them; kind 0x11 is a branch, opcode 0x0f conditional (dword +0x20 set) and 0x10 unconditional (+0x20 zero). So the "kind 0x11 node whose word +0x20 is 0" of the rules above is an unconditional jump: a tail that starts a block entered only by jumps merges at once, and through the swap path when that block is B's.

`return true;` written as a separate statement is `mov al,1` and a jump to the epilogue. Two such returns that each follow a `jne` to the same label share a 13-byte tail (`jne`, `mov al,1`, `jmp`), which is below the threshold on its own. `UIItemWnd::Sort` and `UIItemStoreWnd::Sort` share one such return in the original: the `itemType == 5 || 9 || 8` return was redirected into the `itemType == 0` return (its `mov al,1` carries that line, 4871). The tail-merge routine does not do it. The original's tab `case 1:` ends in `return false;`, not `break;` (verified in both functions). The second `mov al,0; jmp` block is handled by the jump-with-fall-through routine below, and the later relayout gives the shared return.

### Jumps joined with the code that falls into their target

For each unconditional jump, the branch pass calls 0x107295a5. It walks backwards from the jump and from the fall-through predecessor of the target label while the instructions are equal (0x107291dc), stepping over labels, and moves the jump up past the equal instructions (0x10729ba9; 0x1073bf54 first when the walk passed a label). Unlike tail merging, it has no size threshold: one equal instruction is enough.

Folding `jcc T; jmp F; T:` into the inverted `jcc F` (0x10712450) runs in the early branch pass (0x10713d80) and in the branch pass. It needs the first real node after the `jcc` to be the `jmp`, so a label in between blocks it, and it needs T before any real node after the `jmp`. The folded jump takes the `jmp`'s line, so a final `jne` that carries the line of a later `break` was folded from that `break`'s jump.

A label whose first real node is an unconditional jump has all its references retargeted (0x1070f864), unless the jump or one reference has flag 8 at byte +9, which the tuple builder (0x10702160) sets for IL opcodes 0x187 to 0x18c. So a conditional jump that goes to the function's final `return` rather than to a nearer, identical `return 0;` copy comes from a branch whose false path is a `break`: in `UIAuctionWnd::SendMsg`'s `ID_ADD_AUCTION` the last test is `if (g_session.m_waitForAddAuction) { ...; return 0; }` followed by `break;`, not an `else if` before a shared `return 0;`.

### Which copy a tail merge keeps

The kept copy follows the order of the label's reference list. A new reference goes to the head of the list (0x107023f4), so jumps created in source order stand last-first, and the driver takes B from the head: the last copy in the source survives. Moving a label's references to another label (0x1070f880, called by the retarget of a label whose first real node is an unconditional jump, and by the merge of adjacent labels) takes them from the head one at a time and reverses them.

A `switch` without a `default:` has two labels at its end, the one its `break`s jump to and the one the dispatch's out-of-range jump goes to; joining them reverses the breaks once, and a `break;` after the switch (the label is then followed by a jump) reverses them again. With `default: break;` there is one label and one reversal, so the first copy survives.

`UIMessengerGroupWnd::SendMsg`: the `FRIEND_INFO` destructor exits of `switch (val1)` end in the same expanded `_Tidy` tail. The original keeps the copy at `ID_FRIEND_INFO` (the first), the later cases jump into it, the jump from `ID_LEAVE` carries the line of its `break;`, and the `operator delete` arm carries the line of the first `break` inside `ID_DELETE_FRIEND`. Without the `default:` the copy at `ID_LEAVE` is kept; `return 0;` after the switch gives the original's order but separate return blocks (line records of that `return`). The signs: jump targets that differ under equal instruction text, a kept copy at the first exit, and line records of a `break` on jumps into another case. Flag 8, which blocks the retarget, is set at node creation for opcodes 0x187 to 0x18c (0x10702219), and no jump of this function has it.

A function with one exit label (`goto done;` in place of the inner `return`s) makes c2 keep the first copy of each repeated tail and jump back to it. `CommPipeCallback` (GameGuard) matches only in that form: with a `return true;` in every case it keeps the last copies instead (912 bytes against the original's 848).

### Jump threading

A path on which an earlier branch already decides a later test is threaded past that test: the `else` of `if (vertexFog)` jumps straight to the code after `if (vertexFog && useRange)` and gets its own copy of that code and the epilogue. The callee-saved pushes and pops then move too, since the block has two exits. A member self-assignment (`m_fogChanged = m_fogChanged;`) at the join in front of the test keeps the path joining: it leaves no code but is still there when c2 threads. A self-assignment of a local or a global does not, and neither do labels, `goto`s or empty blocks. `CRenderer::DoFog` matches with it and `fogend = ((FARPLANE - NEARPLANE) * g_fogPara.end) + NEARPLANE;`, whose copy node fixes the remaining x87 schedule. Such a statement can be swept over every statement boundary of a procedure.

## Switch lowering, read from c2

c2 lowers a `switch` from a sorted list of nodes. A node is a run of consecutive case values that branch to the same label. Cases whose label is the switch's exit (a group that is only `break;`, with or without a `default:`) are not in the list. Two source groups with identical bodies keep separate labels at this point, even when tail merging later gives them one copy of code and one jump-table slot. The decision function (0x1074db6e) takes the list and its node count, emits one jump table when the list is dense enough, and otherwise asks the chooser (0x10763d7d, through 0x10763d39) for a split, emits `cmp`/`jg`/`je` against that node and recurses on both sides.

The chooser grows a cluster from the first node. It adds the next node while the gap from the previous node's low value is at most T, or larger than the gap after it, and while the node count times T covers the span. If the cluster holds L nodes or fewer (or all of them), the split is the median node, index n/2. Otherwise the split is the first node after the cluster. T is 3 and L is 4 under the project's flags (table 0x107a3280, selected by the global at 0x107ac0b4; L is 8 when that global is 0).

So the root of a large, sparse switch is the median node, and the grouping of case labels moves it even when behaviour and final code are identical. `CSession::GetSkillActionInfo` (432 labels) splits at 383 where the original splits at 384. Moving `SKID_AL_DEMONBANE` from its own `beginEffectId = EF_BEGINSPELL; break;` group into the `SKID_AL_DP` group with the same body merges three nodes into one and matches.

The list c2 gets can be read by tracing a compile with breakpoints at:

- `0x1074db6e` (`[esp+4]`, `[ecx+8]`): each call's node count and first value;
- `0x1074dffa` (`[ecx+8]`, `[ecx+0x10]`, `[ecx+0x18]`): the nodes (low value, high value, label);
- `0x10763d69` (`[ecx]`): the chosen index.

Counting nodes on each side of the original's pivots narrows down which groups the original wrote together.

Case bodies that load the same value into a scratch register take eax, ecx and edx in turn, in source order, and the switch's dispatch code (eax, then ecx for the table index) restarts the turn at edx. A case group whose body equals another's (the same pooled string, for example) disappears from the layout but can still take a turn at its source position. `JT_PORING`, whose body equals the code after the switch, takes one only when it follows an edx body. A body sequence that drifts by one register therefore points at a merged-away case group. `CSession::GetPetIllustName` needs a separate `case JT_LUNATIC:` before `JT_MUNAK`, with `JT_ECLIPSE_P` keeping the same string in its own group at the end, and `JT_PORING` before `JT_ROCKER`. Such placements are found by compiling the moved group at every position and comparing the per-body register sequence with the original's.

## Instruction scheduling, read from c2

The scheduler is pass `0x107258b7`, run after tail merging and before emission. It splits the code into regions and schedules each one alone: it builds a dependence graph (`0x10726014`), computes priorities (`0x10726e85`) and list-schedules the region (`0x10727791`). The project's flags select CPU 5 (`[0x107ac0b0]`, the Pentium row of the weight table `0x107a0d98`).

- A region (`0x10725e50`) runs from the instruction after the previous region to the first instruction of kind (byte `+8`) 0x11, 0x13 or 0x1a, inclusive; it stops before kind 0x18; and it holds at most 81 instructions. A call does not end a region. At the cap, an instruction that would otherwise be scheduled in the same region goes to the next one, behind that region's higher-priority instructions.
  - `C3dGrannyGameActor::RenderNormal` has its `add esp,4` after a `sqrt` call land behind the following `fdivr` when its region holds one node more than the original's.
  - `CTexture::CreateBump(DWORD, DWORD)` has the reverse: its loop latch schedules differently when the original's loop-body region holds one node more, and `FLOAT fy = (y/(FLOAT)ddsd.dwHeight) - 0.5f;` supplies it.

  Compiling with copies of `c2.dll` patched to caps 0x4f and 0x51 shows the direction: if one of them gives the original's bytes, the original's region had one node more or fewer.
- Opcodes from 0x145 up are pseudo-instructions without bytes; they still count toward the cap and take a issue slot (mnemonics for 0 to 0x144 are the pointer table at `0x107a5a98`). A parenthesised float expression is one: C1XX emits IL token 0x59 after it, and c2's IL reader (`0x1072ee79`) turns that into a float copy, opcode 0x162, which copy propagation removes only in some places (a copy feeding a float-to-int conversion goes; one feeding an `fmul` stays). Parentheses around an integer expression emit no token. A functional cast `float(a + b)` or `static_cast<float>` groups without the token; a C cast `(float)(a + b)` keeps it.
- Each copy also creates a temporary, which shifts the numbers of all later temporaries. Commutative-sort keys use a temporary's number mod 4, so removing one copy can reorder an unrelated sum further on. `RenderNormal` needs both: `float(m_fCurRot + 180.0f) * pi / 180.0f` (no copy in the sqrt region) and `ltm.MakeXRotation((pi * 0.5f))` in the preceding `if` (one copy back, in a region where it costs nothing).
- Height (node `+0x34`) is the longest latency path to the region's end plus 1. A node's priority (`+0x2c`) is `height << 13`, plus `1 << 16` for byte `+0x3a` bit 0 (set on instructions with a memory operand), plus word `+0x22` (a use count) `>> 5`. Ready nodes are kept in descending priority; equal priorities take the lower sequence number (`+0x36`, the pre-schedule order) first (insertion at `0x10727948`).
- Each cycle `0x10727977` walks the ready list. The unit class is byte `+0x39` & 7. A class is free while its busy counter (`[0x107991d8 + 12*class]`) is below its capacity, and a node is issued when its ready cycle (`+0x30`) is at most the cycle plus the class's positive busy count. The second instruction of a cycle pairs only by opcode class (`[0x10799278 + 12*op + 8]`: 0 either pipe, 0x100 first only, 0x200 second only). An `fmul` blocks the next `fmul` for two cycles (`[0x107ac2d8]`). A write-after-read edge has latency 0, so `mov edi,eax` and a following `mov eax,[mem]` dispatch in the same cycle.
- Self-moves (`mov eax,eax`, from a register piece that received a value already in the same register) are scheduled like any instruction and deleted afterwards. Spelling changes inside a statement rarely reach the scheduler: c2 normalises them before it. Two instruction-identical compiles that schedule differently have different graphs, priorities or pseudo-instructions; count the pseudo-instructions per region first.
- To read a region's decisions, trace c2 with breakpoints at `0x10731d26` (`[[ecx]+0x18]`, the function name), `0x10727791` (`[ecx]`, `[ecx+4]`; each hit starts a region) and `0x10727a58` (one issued instruction: opcode `[[esi+0x1c]+4]`, priority `[esi+0x2c]`, ready cycle `[esi+0x30]`, height word `[esi+0x34]`, issue cycle `edi` and unit class byte `[esi+0x39]`). `0x10727948` (`[ecx+...]`) lists ready-list insertions. A successor list starts at node `+0xc` (entry: next `+0`, successor node `+0xc`, latency word `+0x14`). To test a hypothesis about the cap or a priority, patch a copy of `c2.dll` (for example the cap byte at `0x10725e64`) and compile with it.

`C3dGrannyGameActor::RenderShadow` and `CGrannyPc::RenderShadow`: the original's `fsubp` issues before `add edi,eax` because the dot loop's float chains carry copies from parenthesised products, `int((vShadow.x * cosDeg) - (vShadow.y * sinDeg) + 17.0f)`. The copies take issue slots and delay `mov eax,[addx]` by one cycle. Fifteen spellings without parentheses schedule identically. The original's line records (the instructions from `faddp` to the loop test attributed to the following `for` line) also show that `dotX` and `dotY` were assigned and then incremented by `addx` and `addy` in separate statements.

## Register candidate numbers, read from c2

A register candidate (a symbol's web or split piece) is made by 0x107119e1. A new node takes the next number from the counter at 0x1079d74c; a freed node goes on a LIFO free list (head 0x107ae038, link +0x2c) and keeps its old number (+0x1c) when 0x10711a78 reuses it. Numbers are therefore recycled: nodes freed in ascending order come back in descending order.

When the colouring pass splits a candidate, c2 rebuilds the pieces of the symbols in the split region, in ascending order of their old numbers, and each new piece pops the free list. The relative order of two symbols' numbers therefore flips at each rebuild that covers both. Block-entry reloads of equal scheduling priority have so far come out in ascending candidate number, but the pass that inserts them is not decoded (not 0x10711a9c or 0x1073e222). If the observation holds, the order of two reloads tells whether the original rebuilt them an even or odd number of times. `CGrannyPc::RenderAndAni`: the split of `this` (pick 18) rebuilds i (old 0x2c) before nPartMeshIdx (old 0x36), giving i 0x5f and nPartMeshIdx 0x25. Ours reloads nPartMeshIdx first; the original reloads i first.

The first numbers come from the same free list (traced on `CTexture::CreateBump(w, h, surface)`). c2 first makes one candidate per use of the constant 0 and frees the single-use ones; the symbols' webs then pop that list in ascending symbol number, and take fresh ascending numbers once it is empty. A symbol's number follows its first reference in the IL: a declaration with an initialiser is a reference, a declaration without one is not. A local's place in the pop order, and so its candidate number, therefore moves with a dead initialiser and with nothing else that leaves the code alone (539 dead reads and 132 declaration placements change nothing). In `CreateBump` the pops are `this` c17, `pSurface` c22, ..., `pDst` c9, `y` c23; the eviction split (below) makes the pieces in that ascending order, and the loads after the inner loop come out `this`, `pSurface`, `y` against the original's `y`, `this`, `pSurface`. `DWORD y = 0;` among the declarations before `pDst`, with `for (y = 0; ...)`, gives `y` c9 and the original's order; the first store is dead and leaves no code. The reloads keep the order of the split that first made them, although a later split rebuilds `this` and `pSurface`.

Trace breakpoints: `0x10711a10` (`eax`, `[edi+0x1c]`, byte `[edi+4]`) for new numbers, `0x10711a78` (`[esi+0x1c]`, `[edi+0x1c]`, byte `[edi+4]`) for reused numbers and `0x1071c157` (`[esi+0x1c]`, `[[esi]+0x1c]`) for picks, each with the candidate's symbol number.

### Priority

A candidate's priority (`+0x3c`) is summed by the weighing pass (`0x1071d925`) per tuple, each term multiplied by the block weight `1 << [block+0x6e]` (1 outside loops, 2 in a loop, 4 in a nested loop). The terms are added at `0x1071de72` and `0x1071e0db` (a reference's benefit, which also counts references) and subtracted at `0x1071db8e` (pseudo-op 0x164) and `0x1071dd1d` (pseudo-op 0x163).

A constant (kind 13; symbol 0 is the constant 0) is weighed as follows:

- each materialisation (0x163, at the piece's entry and at each loop head it re-enters) costs 1;
- `push 0`, `cmp x, 0` and `mov reg, 0` gain 0;
- a `mov [mem], 0` usually gains 1, though the same store gained 0 in another weighing.

A piece with priority 0 or below gets no register, and the constant then stays an immediate. A zero register therefore comes from zero stores to memory, not from zero arguments. In `UISelectCharWnd::OnDraw` two stores in a loop outweigh the entry and loop-head materialisations (-1 -2 +2 +2 = 1, ebx). In the original, the split had put those stores into a piece of their own, which took eax.

### Sort key

The sort key (`+0xc`) is built by the same pass, block by block (`0x1071da32` to `0x1071e25e`). For each block, the pressure is the number of candidates referenced in it (the members of a set, the entries of a second list, and the flagged entries of the class's live list) times the block weight. A candidate referenced in the block gets `key += pressure * benefit`, where the benefit (`+0x18`) is the priority it gained in that block; a candidate that is only live through the block gets `key -= pressure`. A candidate therefore sorts early when its references sit in blocks that reference many other candidates, and late when it is live through many or busy blocks. Consequences:

- A constant used at least twice in a region is a candidate whose range starts at its first use, or in the loop's preheader when that use is inside a loop. The start counts as a reference with benefit 0, so it raises the pressure of that block without any code. `UIWindowMgr::Aco_Travel`: the block before the first item loop has pressure 10, because the second loop reuses four constants of the first (the format string, `0xffff00`, `&g_windowMgr`, a stored zero) and their ranges start there; the same statements before the third loop, which has no second loop after it, have pressure 5. `numItem` is defined in that block with benefit 4, so its key is -33 in the first loop and -54 in the third, and in the third a piece of the zero constant (key -37) takes `ebx` first.
- A piece made by a split is weighed again and enters the same sorted list, so it can be coloured before a whole candidate of a lower key.
- Nonzero constants follow the same rules, read from c2 on the 0x7eb of `CGameGuard::PreInitNPGameMonI`. The allocator's entry (`0x10714f8f`) clears a 64-bucket table (`0x1079d750`) once per function and walks every tuple (`0x10715200`). Each constant operand (node kind 7) of a tuple that `0x10717cc2` accepts goes to `0x10717d90`, which finds or makes the constant's symbol (kind 13) by node kind and low dword, whatever its type, and replaces the operand with a reference to it. A second sighting anywhere in the function sets bit 0x10 of the symbol's byte +5; without it the operand is turned back into an immediate (`0x10711a9c`, called from `0x10715ba8`). Loads (pseudo-op 0x163, made by `0x10711ae3`) are placed by availability (`0x10715f90`): one before each use that no use precedes on every path, or in the loop's preheader. A load followed by a single use is removed again before the first weighing (`0x1073a4b6`).

  A reference gains 1 when the tuple's other operand is memory (`0x1071e4f3`: node kind 2, or kind 6 with a base or index; a store, `cmp [mem], c`, `add [mem], c`) and 0 when it is a register or the compare is against 0. So one store keeps its immediate, two stores in one web take a register, and a single reference takes one only in a loop, where its weight is 2 against the preheader's 1 (the `add [mem], 2` of this function's hex loops). Measured with a second store of 0x7eb:
  - in the same block: `mov eax, 0x7eb` and two register stores;
  - in a dominating block: one web of priority 1 that loses the colouring across the inline `strlen` and is reverted (`0x1073dee7`);
  - behind the call: a split into single-use pieces of priority 0;
  - in a sibling arm: a load for each use, and both reverted.

  Nothing that leaves no code adds a counted reference: a use that c2 folds before the walk leaves no sighting (a dead or overwritten store, the same store in both arms of an `if`/`else`, also through `volatile`, `__assume`), and the copies of a statement in sibling arms, which tail merging joins after the colouring, are separate webs. A register constant with one store therefore is not a constant candidate; see the header copy under "Source forms that changed codegen". Trace: breakpoint `0x10717d90` with `[edx+0x18]`, `[ecx+4]`, `[ecx+0x10]` for the sightings (value, opcode, line in the low word), and `[[[esi]+0x28]+0x18]` for the value at the weighing's breaks.
- The candidate numbers of this pass are not the numbers seen at the picks; match candidates by symbol, key and block count.

### The split of candidates left without a register

When a pick takes the last register open to a neighbour, the neighbour is left for the split (neighbour update `0x1071fa67`; left neighbours that need a byte register and are not constants are split first, on their own, against the byte register set `[0x107ac190]`). `0x1073e62a` then places kind-0x1b marker tuples, and the split proper (`0x1073d0c5`) cuts each left candidate into pieces that never cross a marker. The pieces are weighed again and relisted. The markers are placed in one of two modes:

- Region mode, when three or more candidates are left and `[0x1079e88c]` is at least 100 (`cmp dword [0x1079e88c], 0x64` at `0x1073e64a`; a compiler copy patched to another threshold forces either mode). `[0x1079e88c]` is the function's block count at that point, one per block in the loop at `0x1071cd8d`; `UISelectCharWnd::OnDraw` has 113 there and 101 blocks in the final code. Every block that holds a left candidate is taken in layout order. A block with no free register is in no region. Otherwise it joins the region of the first predecessor (then successor) for which a register free in the block is not yet in use anywhere in that region; failing that, it starts a region. Use is counted per block, so a register whose holder dies before the candidate's definition in the same block still counts as used. Markers holding the whole left set go on the region boundaries. Inside a region a piece runs from its first reference after a marker to its last, so a stretch the candidate is only live through gets no register.
- Per-candidate mode (`0x1073ea5f`, block walker `0x1073ecd0`), with fewer than three left. From the candidate's first block the walk goes breadth-first along the successors where the candidate is live, tuple by tuple, with one running set of free registers shared by the whole walk. A block in which the set would empty gets the marker and is not continued. The set only shrinks, so the piece ends at the first place, in breadth-first order, where its last surviving register is taken.

Region assignment and marker placement in region mode (`0x1073e780` to `0x1073ea52`):

- A block looks for a region among its predecessors in list order, skipping those that have none yet, and takes the first whose region leaves it a register (free in the block, not in use in the region). When no predecessor serves, the successors are tried the same way; failing that, the block starts a region.

  The predecessor list comes from the flow-graph builder (`0x10702c44`, edge constructor `0x10702c73`, which prepends): blocks are taken in layout order, each first gets the fall-through edge from the block before it (unless that block ends in an unconditional jump), then one edge per entry of its label's reference list `[label+0x1c]`. A predecessor list is therefore the label's references in reverse list order with the fall-through predecessor always last, and which jump a source form makes first decides the region: `[b97, b91, b10, b9, b109 fall-through]` with `continue` in the last `if` of a loop body, `[b91, b10, b9, b109, b106]` with that `if` as the head of the closing `else if` chain.
- A block joins a region only through a register that is free in the block and unused in the region, and that register stays free after the join, so every region keeps at least one register free throughout. A block with nothing in use (a loop latch) therefore always joins the first predecessor in list order that has a region.
- Markers then go block by block. A block in no region gets one at its start and one at its end. For a block in region r: if every predecessor is outside r, a marker at the block's start; if only some are, a marker at the end of each outside predecessor. If every successor is outside r, a marker at the block's end; if only some are, a marker at the start of each outside successor. A marker at a block's end sits before the branch and kills the flow to every successor, also the ones in the same region.
- A marker does not by itself cost a load. In `UISelectCharWnd::OnDraw` the first `empty()` test ends with a marker, and the second test goes on using `esi` and takes `this` by `mov eax, ecx`; loads appear on the edges where the candidate has no register (an observation; the pass that inserts them is not decoded).
- `UISelectCharWnd::OnDraw`: with the i loop's body ending `if (DoubleAcc == 100) { DoubleAcc = 0; continue; } if (i == 4) ... else if (i == 3) ...`, the `continue` makes the `DoubleAcc = 0` block the first predecessor of the i latch. That block follows a block with no free register, so it starts a region of its own, and the latch joins it and leaves the region of the rest of the loop. The markers around the latch then cut `this`, `j`, the zero constant and `curChar` differently (336 differing lines). The original's line table shows the jump after `DoubleAcc = 0` and the `i == 4` test under one record, that is `if (DoubleAcc == 100) DoubleAcc = 0; else if (i == 4) ... else if (i == 3) ...`; with it the latch's list starts with a block of the main region, both latches join that region, and the function is identical. Moving the `Not Available` call into an else branch gives 81 lines by putting the j latch into the i latch's stray region, and is not the original's form.
- A block that contains an inlined `memcpy` (`rep movs`: `esi`, `edi`, `ecx`) and a call (`eax`, `ecx`, `edx`) has no free register next to `this` in `ebx`, joins no region and gets a marker on both sides, so a candidate left at the first pick is cut at every such block. A piece that still reaches a later use without passing one is live across calls and takes a callee-saved register; a piece whose remaining uses all lie behind one is a one-block candidate and takes `ecx` for a `this` argument.

  In `UIChatRoomWnd::SendMsg` the address of `m_menuItems` is `lea esi` plus `mov ecx, esi` at both `push_back`s of the member switch's second case and `lea ecx` in the else branch. With the `MSI_WRITE_CHAT_TO_FILE` `push_back` after the if/else, every path meets its string's `memcpy` block and every piece is one-block; the original has it inside the else, so the case's pieces reach `m_menuItems.size()` and take `esi` (and `menu` then takes `edi`). The same move fixes the argument order of the `memberName != myName` compares, whose temporaries are placed after the global candidates. The source evidence is a jump target: the `memberName` destructor at the end of the if branch jumps into the tail of the `WRITE_CHAT` statement, past its `push_back`. Diffs that mask relocations and branch targets hide exactly this; a jump whose distance differs by more than the size shift is a structural difference.

  `CGameGuard::PreInitNPGameMonI` is the same case with nothing in the bytes to show it: the inlined `CLog::Open` with `strcpy` and `CreateFileA` in one such block does not match; the original has the copy in each arm of the test before it (the copies then leave `edx` free, the call's block `esi` and `edi`), and the tail merge gives the same code either way. When a function keeps values in memory around a copy-and-call block, try the copy in the arms.

A split also happens with no candidate left. Before the chooser runs, `0x1071ea4d` walks every tuple of the picked candidate's blocks backwards and counts the registers the tuple needs: the registers live there, the candidate itself, and the uncoloured candidates the tuple references (a candidate that needs a byte register, flag 0x80 at `+5`, is counted in a second total). When the count reaches the class's register count (`0x1071f220`, 6 here), `0x1073e375` puts a marker before the tuple that holds every candidate live there and not referenced by it. Those candidates are the pick's split set (third argument of `0x1071fa67`), so they are cut around that tuple before any of them is coloured.

The count depends on the order of the tuples: in `CreateBump`, `iDv = *pSrcB2 - *pSrcB1` reaches 6 at the pick of `v01` only when the second load defines `iDv` while the first load's temporary is live. That is the order the original has, and it needs `pSrcB1` to sort before `pSrcB2` by the key of a load through a pointer local (the pointer's number & 7; see the sort-key items under "Reading the original before searching"): `pSrcB1` assigned first, and one more local referenced before it (`HRESULT hr` taken from `CreateSurface`). Without the eviction `pDstT` is coloured whole and takes `edi`.

A candidate whose priority is zero or below is first moved to the end of the list (flag 0x40 at `+5`, `0x10720689`). On its second visit `0x10720538` splits it on its own:

- when it has a pseudo-load (0x163) whose operand carries flag 0x80 (`+6` bit 0, set at `0x1071dd3b`), `0x1073e2a9` deletes that load and puts a marker at the start of each successor of its block where the candidate is live;
- otherwise, when it spans several blocks and `+7` is positive (`+7` counts store-backs 0x164 and tuples that name the candidate twice, `0x1071dba1` and `0x1071dbe7`), `0x10743cf3` puts a marker at the start of every block where it is live, which leaves one-block pieces;
- otherwise it stays without a register.

The pick loop (`0x1071c157`) sends a negative priority there, and priority 0 only when the candidate spans more than one block (`+0x28` differs from `+0x30`) or is a constant; a one-block variable of priority 0 gets no register. A candidate with the load flag goes to the load deletion on its first visit. Without the flag, `0x107218d1` first adds the cost of one load (`0x1071e27e`: 2 for a variable, 1 for a constant) to the priority and, when the sum is positive, keeps one entry of the list at `+0x34` (the largest `+8` among those in the set at `+0x20`) and sets the priority to 1 (read, not traced on a case). The benefit of a reference is 0 or 1 (`0x1071e298`, times the cost and the block weight): 0 for an operand with bit 0x20 of byte `+0x10`, which `this` carries as the base of a member access. A split changes only a candidate's loads; its references and store-backs stay. An address-taken local (`if (0) f(&v);`) is cut at the calls before the allocation, so its candidates hold call-free stretches only.

The local pass `0x1073d33d` and the solver `0x1073d5fc` compute, per left candidate, live = UE | (out - A) backwards and reach = ((in - A) | F) & live forwards; a marker counts as a kill (A).

## Alias classes of indirections, read from c2

Whether a load may be scheduled ahead of an earlier store is an alias question, and its answer can hang on one statement elsewhere in the function. In `UIWindowMgr::MakeWindow` the original has, at all 53 inlined `AddWindow(wnd)` sites,

    lea ecx,[esi+0x34]; mov [ebp+8],edi; lea edx,[ebp+8]; mov eax,[ecx+4]

where a compile with a function-scope temporary (below) has `mov eax,[esi+0x38]; lea ecx,[esi+0x34]; lea edx,[ebp+8]; mov [ebp+8],edi`. The store fills the inlined parameter's temporary, whose address goes to `list::insert`; the load is `_Head`. In the original the load waits for the store and is therefore issued after the `lea`, which keeps the address unfolded.

- The scheduler's dependence builder (`0x10726c72`, oracle `0x1070438b`) makes a memory operand wait for a pending one only when they may alias. A stack symbol with its address taken (`[sym+5] & 2`) against an indirection of class C is `0x10704bac(sym, C)`: class 1 aliases everything; otherwise the symbol must be in the class's list at `[0x1079d6b0][C]` (directly, or in a set of kind 0xc with members at `+0x30`). Once stack packing has run (`[0x107adfe0] = 1`, set at `0x10722117`), a symbol also aliases whatever any symbol sharing its packed slot aliases (`0x1073abb5`, list `0x107ae23c`).
- Symbol bits at `+5`: 2 address taken, 4 the address is passed or stored (`0x10735db4`: an address-of operand of a tuple whose destination is not a tracked pointer), 8 not address-taken local or parameter (trackable), 0x20 packed. The pass at `0x10732f12` gives each symbol a class number from the counter at `0x1079d670` and puts address-taken parameters and the function's globals into the set that outside pointers reach.
- Every block with declarations and every inline expansion is bracketed by scope tuples (opcode 0x1b4, end 0x1b5; the tuple's `+0x10` is 0x10000 + the line relative to the function's first line). A scope that lists address-taken symbols opens a new set: those symbols plus the enclosing scope's (`0x10733c27`). The class of an indirection comes from its root pointer symbol and the current scope (`0x10751556`, `0x107516d7`, `0x107698b4`).
- Measured in `MakeWindow`: with the natural source the `_Head` load is class 1 and waits for the store; with a compiler temporary bound to a reference parameter anywhere at function scope it is class 3, whose list holds only the globals, and the load goes first at every site. `m_children.push_back(wnd)` with a `UIFrameWnd*` in `WID_SAYDIALOGWND` makes such a temporary; an unrelated `void f(UIWindow *const &); f(wnd);` in another case does the same, and a named local in a block (`{ UIWindow *child = wnd; f(child); }`) does not. Which step of the class assignment the function-scope temporary changes is not decoded.
- A c2 patched so that every address-taken local aliases every class (`e9 7d 00 00 00` at `0x10704cad`) gives the original's order at all sites: use such a patch to test an alias hypothesis before searching for its source.
- The counter at `0x1079d670` also numbers the classes derived per pointer and scope, and `0x10751759` gives class 1 to every class it would number 0x400 or higher. A function with about a thousand symbols (locals, temporaries, the copies of every inline expansion) therefore loses pointer-specific classes from some point of the walk on: with the `push_back` temporary in place, 256 to 258 dead locals give the original's order at all 53 sites, fewer leave the first sites folded, and 260 or more change other code.
- The symbols of the last expansion of an inline callee in a function come early in the stack-symbol order (measured: index 0x5a for its argument temporary, where the other sites' follow the function's own); they do not join the parameter pre-slot and get ordinary slots. Why the last expansion differs is not decoded. The original shows which site that is: in `MakeWindow` the temporary at `[ebp-0x10]` beside `width` belongs to `WID_SAYDIALOGWND`, where `AddWindow` is expanded and its `push_back` stays a call, so that site is an `AddWindow(wnd)` like the others and the budget ends inside it.

## Twin arms

A steering device for the candidates' sort keys, to be tried after the natural forms. An if/else whose two arms are the same read through a volatile-qualified cast,

    short story;
    if (g_curStance > 0)
        story = *(volatile short*)&g_curStory;
    else
        story = *(volatile short*)&g_curStory;

stays two blocks while c2 weighs and colours the candidates and leaves the code of one read: tail merging joins the arms afterwards, and the test leaves nothing when the flags it needs are already set. The two arm blocks and their references move the keys of every candidate referenced in them or live through them (see "Sort key": `key += pressure * benefit`, `key -= pressure`), and with the keys the order of the picks. The project counts every function whose body spells `volatile` as steered (twin arms), and callers that inline it as well; no review exempts it. Measured on `UIWindowMgr::Aco_Travel`:

- The test is free only on the false side of an equality test of the same operand (`if (x == 0) ... else` followed by `x > 0`: c2 emits one compare and `je`/`jg`). One relational test per site; a third arm emits the compare again. On the true side, or with another operand, the test stays in the code.
- The arms must hold something c2 cannot fold: a volatile read or a call. Plain copies (`story = g_curStory;` in both arms, or a declaration with that initialiser) are folded before the weighing and change nothing; identities and booleans materialise (`sete cl; test cl, cl`, `lea ecx, [eax - 1]`).
- When the arms differ before the common statement, the plain read survives without `volatile`: `if (g_curStance == 0) { g_menuFreeze = 0; map = map_Number; } else { map = map_Number; }` followed by `if (map == 1)` compiles to the code of `if (g_curStance == 0) g_menuFreeze = 0; if (map_Number == 1)`, with one more block for the candidates that are live there. A volatile read in only one of the two arms changes the code.
- An instruction that heads every arm is hoisted above the branch (`test eax, eax; mov ax, [map]; jne`), so a store written before the common read in one arm ends up after it.
- With three arms (`== 0`, `> 0`, else) a zero store in the first arm is written from the register that holds the tested zero (`mov [g_menuFreeze], ax`) instead of an immediate; no spelling of the test or of the store changes that.
- Copies of a statement that reads a constant-propagated local do not merge (`waitTime` in `timeGetTime() + waitTime` becomes `add eax, 0x9c4` in one copy). A local that carries a call's result into the merged tail turns `add eax, ecx` into `movsx ecx, [w]; add ecx, eax`.
- A statement written in both arms of the test that follows it, instead of once before it, leaves the block before the test: `if (g_curStory == 52) { waitTime = 1000; ... } else { waitTime = 1000; }` after a call compiles to the same `cmp; mov; jne` as `waitTime = 1000; if (g_curStory == 52) { ... }`, but the call's block no longer references `waitTime`, so its pressure drops by one and the key of each candidate referenced there by its benefit.

`UIWindowMgr::Aco_Travel` is identical with markers. Map 2's header keeps the 16-bit constant 42 in `ebx` (`mov ebx, 0x2a; cmp word [g_curStory], bx`) and reloads `gameMode` from its home for the `Zc_Notify_Entry_HP` call. Without the markers `gameMode`'s piece takes `ebx` there: the piece (the function's first block, map 1's, map 2's and map 3's notify blocks and the blocks between) has key 6, the constant key 3 (header +9, six live-through tests -9, the store block +3), so the piece is picked first. Three changes bring the piece to key 2 and the constant first:

- the `waitTime = 1000` of map 3's notify block in both arms of the story-52 test (key 4);
- the read of `map_Number` in both arms of the function's first stance test (key 2);
- a twin arm on `g_curStory` ahead of map 3's story chain, which gives the third item loop of map 3 the original's registers (the zero constant in no register, the item count in `ebx`).

None of the three leaves code. Forms that do not reach the original: twins in the tails (`if (boy)`), in the final else of story 41, on `timeGetTime()` with a carried result, `waitTime` with its address taken, and 16 symbol shifts.

## Source forms that changed codegen

Each form below was, by itself, the change that made a procedure match.

- A statement written twice and merged by c2: `isStrDifferenti` matches with `++left; ++right; continue;` in the equal-characters arm and the same increments at the end of the loop body. Operand order, `&p[i]` against `p + i`, `for` against `while`, casts and declaration order do not change its code or `SeedEncrypt`'s.
- Statements the original wrote as one expression: `a = a + r - 30;` and `a += r; a -= 30;` compile differently (`CEffectPrim::PrimTeiHit2`).
- A separate counter next to the loop index (`k = -1; i = 0; while (i < n) { k++; i++; ... a[k] }`) gives the strength-reduced pointer plus counter; the declaration order of the two locals changes the instruction order (`CRagEffect::StatusState`).
- `a[2] = a[1] = a[0] = -1;` stores in ascending order.
- A function's result used directly as an initializer instead of through a second statement changes its IL size, and with it the inline candidate flag (`CInsultFilter::IsGoodSentence`).
- An inline overload that forwards to the same call gives the same instructions but different IL, and with it different register preferences. `UIBookWnd::SendMsg`: `ITEM_INFO::GetItemDesc(atoi((const char*)val1))` keeps the title in eax and the inline strcmp's byte in cl; `ITEM_INFO::GetItemDesc((const char*)val1)`, through the `const char*` overload, gives the original's ecx and al.
- An argument computed in its own statement comes before the virtual call's vtable load in c2's instruction list. Computed inside the call, it comes after, which changes the scratch rotation and the scheduling. `UIItemCollectionWnd::SendMsg`: `int isCardItem = IsCardItem(...); m_cardItemButton->Move(6, isCardItem * 100 - 94);` loads the vtable first. `int cardY = IsCardItem(...) * 100 - 94; m_cardItemButton->Move(6, cardY);` gives the original's lea chain through edx, with the vtable load after it. The original's line records cannot tell the two apart, because the record switches to the call's line at the first `this` load.
- A value computed in one register and copied into another at a join (`add eax,ecx; mov ecx,eax`, with the other branch entering at the `mov`) is two variables, one assigned in both branches. `UINewChatWnd::SendMsg`: `int state = v ? -1 : 1; if (m_state) state = m_state + state * 3;` keeps one variable in `ecx`. `int dir = v ? -1 : 1; int state; if (m_state) state = m_state + dir * 3; else state = dir;` gives the original's `eax`/`edx` head and the copy. Copy-only spellings (`int dir = v; ...`, `state = dir;` after the `if`) are coalesced and change nothing.
- The block a local array is declared in changes scheduling around it. `UIAuctionWnd::GetCurAuctionItem`: with `char itemName[25];` at function level, the byte copy `item->m_isIdentified = it->IsIdentified;` issues before the inlined `strlen(itemName)` (its store has a dependence path through the `itemName[24] = 0` store and the scan). Declared inside the `if` that uses it, the copy issues after `repne scasb` as in the original, with the same frame slot. Spellings of the copy and 12 statement orders have no effect. Try the innermost-block declaration for every function-level local of a procedure.
- `c ? 0 : x` as a call argument compiles branchless (`dec`/`neg`/`sbb`/`and`). The original's `push 0; jmp` / `push x` arms jumping into one shared call sequence are an `if`/`else` with two calls that tail merging joined. `CGameActor::Am_Action`: the ternary gives 412 differing lines; `if (count == 1) SendMsg(.., int(&wba)); else SendMsg(.., int(&wba), totalDamage);` gives 108, the rest being two swapped frame slots.
- A sum the original writes twice becomes a CSE temporary, and a temporary sum gets `lea dst,[a+b]`. A named variable assigned the sum is copied from the temporary and keeps a two-address `mov x,t; add x,load`: c2's address-mode routine (entry `0x107185d7`; `lea`, opcode 0x12, stored at `0x10719393` and `0x10719502`) keeps the add when the destination's symbol equals a source's. With the expression repeated, copy propagation also removes the variable, and the PDB has no record for it. `UIWindowMgr::SendMsg`: `int x = info.x + count * 17; ... Move(x, y);` leaves one `mov`/`add`; the original's `Move(info.x + count * 17, info.y + count * 17)` after the bounds test gives the `lea`, and its scope records lack `y`, as in the original. A `lea` against a `mov`/`add` pair, and a PDB scope missing a local, both point at a repeated expression.
- c1 folds every chain of constant factors into one multiply: `X * 15 * 2`, `(X * 15) * 2`, `2 * (X * 15)`, `X * 15 << 1`, `X * 15 + X * 15`, a macro, and an inline function returning `X * 15` all become `X * 30`. Two bounds `X * 30 + 33` and `X * 30 - 27` then share the CSE `X * 30` (`lea`, `lea`, `shl`, then `lea`/`add` per bound). A named local survives c1: with `int x = X * 15;` the shared value is `X * 15`, each `x * 2 + c` (or `x + x + c`) becomes `lea r,[t+t+c]`, and copy propagation removes the local, so the PDB has no record of it. In `UIWindowMgr::BS_Tetris` the original's `lea edi,[edx+edx+0x21]` / `lea edx,[edx+edx-0x1b]` come only from such locals. A `[t+t+c]` in the original against a `shl` in ours means the source held the half value in a variable.
- The order in which locals are first assigned is their symbol order, and with it the order of their frame slots and of the operands of a sum; the declaration order and the spelling of the sum are not. `UISpriteButton::OnDraw`: `oy = ox = h = w = 0;` gives `w` the slot above `ox` and subtracts `w` first in `m_w / 2 - ox - w + clip->x` (108 spellings of the two sums compile alike); `int w = 0, h = 0, ox = 0, oy = 0;` gives the original's slots and order.
- Zero stores written as immediates between a pointer load and its first use (`mov edx,[img]; mov [srcx],0; mov [srcy],0; movsx eax,[edx]`) are stores the scheduler moved up into the address-generation gap, at a point where no register was free for the zero: in the source they follow the loads (`int imgWidth = img->width, imgHeight = img->height, srcx = 0, srcy = 0;`). Written before the loads they get `xor eax,eax` and two register stores.

- A clamp that re-reads the member (`imgWidth += m_w - img->width - x;`) keeps `sub`/`sub`/`add`; with the local (`- imgWidth`) c2 folds it to `imgWidth = m_w - x`.
- `if (p) { if (FAILED(hr = f())) return hr; } else return S_OK;` and `if (p) if (FAILED(hr = f())) return hr; if (!p) return S_OK;` test the same things, since c2 threads the second test, but lay the blocks out differently. - A line record is a statement-start marker kept in statement order; the scheduler moves instructions across markers, and a statement whose instructions all moved behind the next marker has no record. Missing records therefore do not show missing statements, and the instructions under a record need not belong to its line.

When one expansion of several identical ones differs, sweep the count of locals first (every subset of use groups given its own variable).
- A pointer copy (`CMotion* headMotion = mot;`) and its source are not interchangeable at their use sites: where the copy is declared against a call, and which of the two names each use spells, decide which loads take a scratch turn and in what order. `CPc::RenderBodyLayer` (`src/Pc.cpp`): with the copy after the `GetMotion` call the head-layer test takes ecx/edx swapped and the offset statements match; with the copy before the call (or `mot` everywhere) the test matches and the y offset does not. The original is the copy before the call, `mot->attachCnt` in the test, and the copy in the three later uses. Sweep the name per use site (2 placements x 15 masks) when two placements each fix a different half.
- A motion pointer that one branch replaces and then tests is two variables in these sources: the layer's motion, and a branch-local copy that takes the replacement first. `UIEquipWnd::OnDraw` and `UISelCharForUServerWnd::OnDraw`, as in `CPc::RenderBodyLayer`: `CMotion* accMotion = curMotion;` at the top of the accessory branch, `int tmpMotion = ...; curMotion = accMotion = ...->GetMotion(.., tmpMotion);` in the animated case, and `accMotion` in the attach test. With one variable, c2 splits `curMotion` by regions around the animated case and reads it from memory at the test. The copy is a candidate of its own and holds `ebx` from the branch entry to the test. `UISelectCharWnd::OnDraw`'s PDB has a frame record named `accMotion`.
- A slot swap repeated over many unrelated temporaries is a weight change of one named local that shares their slots. In `UIEquipWnd::OnDraw` the same change gives `curMotion` one stack reference fewer (weight 4 to 3), so its slot is opened after `sprRes`'s, and the length and pointer temporaries of thirteen inlined string assignments, which join the newest free slot in turn, swap slots.
- `UISelCharForUServerWnd::OnDraw`'s two function-level statics take the original's order once they have the sibling's initialisers (`static int head_imfOffX = 0;`); uninitialised, they come out swapped.
- `SeedEncrypt` (`src/PacketProtect.cpp`, no markers): the returned length is a variable of its own (`int nRet = 0; ... nRet = nLength; ... nRet += nPad; return nRet + nDone;`), the pad length is a pointer difference against a named block end (`BYTE* pBlock = pbData + nDone; BYTE* pBlockEnd = pBlock + 16; int nPad = pBlockEnd - (pBlock + nLength);`), and both calls spell `pbData + nDone`. Read from c2 on the way:
  - parameters are numbered from right to left and locals by first reference;
  - `lea dst,[a+b]` takes the operand with the higher sort key as its base (`ebp` is never a base without a displacement);
  - the intrinsic `memset` evaluates the count, the value and then the destination, unless a part of the destination is a value of its own with several uses;
  - a pointer expression used once is substituted forward into its use.
- `loadDLL` (`src/SafeAPI.cpp`, no markers, without the `LoadDLLFrame` and `LoadDLLBuffers` structs). A named local for the image size (`int imageSize = calcTotalImageSize(...); mapped = HeapAlloc(GetProcessHeap(), 0, imageSize);`) is one more candidate: with it the `HeapAlloc` import pointer takes `ebp` and `fileSize` stays a memory local, where the nested call leaves `ebp` to `fileSize`. `char fullPath[MAX_PATH + 1]` (0x105, padded to 0x108) is the 4 bytes between `fullPath` and `ext` in the frame (0x284). The operands of the compare follow the source: `bytesRead == fileSize` gives `mov ecx,[fileSize]; mov eax,[bytesRead]; cmp eax,ecx`.
- A constant that reaches its store through a local aggregate is a register temporary, not an immediate (no markers). `CGameGuard::PreInitNPGameMonI`: `Header header = { size, 0x7eb, m_processId }; message.header = header;` compiles to `mov eax, 0x7eb; mov [message.command], eax`, and `memcpy(&message, &header, sizeof(header))` to the same; the other two fields compile as with direct assignments. `UIChatRoomWnd::OnCreate` has it from `int ids2[3] = { ID_CLOSE };` read in a one-trip `for` (`mov ecx, 0x87; mov [eax+0x2c], ecx`). Inferred: c2 forwards the copy after its constant propagation has run. A four-byte struct copy, a `memcpy` from one scalar and a one-element array read in a one-trip `for` give the immediate.

## Reading the original before searching

- A diff score only picks candidates. Statement swaps that lower the score are often contradicted by the original's instruction order (`UIWindowMgr::SendMsg`: the original assigns `String` before `Color` although the swapped order scores 30% better). Confirm every reorder against the original's call and store order.
- The order of two adjacent independent statements can change register allocation for the whole function (`CTexture::CreateBump`: declaring `pSrcB2` before `pSrcB1` gives 284 differing lines where the original's order gives 17).
- A counter the original reloads and stores at the loop end came from a temporary: `int n = a + 1; ... a = n;` beside a separate index variable (`UIEmotionWnd::OnCreate`).
- Local-variable records: a name the original's PDB has and our `/Z7` object lacks is a usable signal; extra names on our side and the order of records are not.
- The unwind map (handler, `FuncInfo`, state count and per-state action) tells how many destructible objects the original had; a state with a null action is removed dead code (below).
- A member function that the class's type record lists but that has no procedure symbol was inline in the original (`CTexture::SetName`).
- Line records with no counterpart in our source are statements, not blanks, when the code around them differs. Measured cases:
  - A statement the optimizer removes still shapes register choice. `m_backB2 = 68;` (a value the member already holds) keeps the 68 constant live so the next constant takes another register (`UIPlayerGage::UIPlayerGage`); `width -= dx; height = m_parentHeight;` in every one-statement `case` changes which variables get callee-saved registers (`UIResizer::OnMouseMove`).
  - `cmp reg, zero-register` where we emit `test reg, reg` on a call result: the result was assigned inside the condition, `(price = atoi(s)) == 0`, to a variable never read (`UIMerchantShopMakeWnd::SendMsg`). Spellings of the constant or the comparison do not produce it.
  - Parameter loads on their own line records before the first test are copies: `int cx = x; int cy = y;` (`CMode::MakeHelpBalloon`).
  - A missing line record between two that carry one arm each can be an identical arm that tail merging removed. `CGameActor::Am_Action`: compares for 0x506 and 0x725 on line 143, 0x507 on 145, the `+408` arm on 146 and nothing on 144: `else if (job == 0x506 || job == 0x725) delayTime += 408; else if (job == 0x507) delayTime += 408;`. The removed arm's load and store still count toward `delayTime`'s frame-slot weight (see the frame-slot rule below), which puts `delayTime` and `count` in the original's slots.
- Full `and reg, 0xff` / `shl` sequences where we get byte-register moves come from the `RGB()` macro on `int` operands (`CMode::MakeHelpBalloon`).
- A commutative-operand tie against a strength-reduced pointer depends on the temporary's number modulo 4 (only 0 sorts below a symbol). An inline accessor in place of the member (`GetWorld()->m_player` for `m_world->m_player`) compiles to the same bytes and adds one temporary; dead stores, fillers and declaration order add none (`CGameMode::Zc_Notify_Groundskill`).
- The instruction diff masks jump targets and frame offsets. When it shows no difference but the procedure does not match, compare bytes with relocation fields masked; two procedures differ only in which copy of a `return true` block two branches target (`UIItemWnd::Sort`).
- Several independent stores whose original order is reproduced by hand-interleaving statements are wrong; scanning the natural groupings byte-exactly finds the order (`CDynamicVB::Init`: the global is read first, then the three members, then the descriptor).
- `P p = {0};` on an aggregate gives per-field zero stores from a zero register; a constructor (initializer list, body assignments or `memset`) gives other stores. A packet struct whose original shows the field-wise pattern had no constructor (`PACKET_CZ_REASSEMBLY_IDENTITY`).
- The original PDB's frame-local records carry scope structure: within one scope they come in descending name-hash bucket (`h = 4h + (h >> 4) + c`, folded `h ^ (h >> 16)`, low 4 bits), oldest declaration first inside a bucket, and scopes follow in preorder. A break in the descending order is a scope boundary, so the record order shows which block a local was declared in and the relative order of two same-bucket declarations (`UIGuildMemberManageWnd::OnDraw`: `itemY` belongs to the loop's else block, not to the function scope; the grid loop therefore has its own counter).

  A frame slot the original writes without any local record is a compiler temporary: when it holds a loop value, the source has a counter and an expression of it (`for (row = 0; row < 6; ++row)` with `row * 35 + 46`), which c2 strength-reduces, not a user variable stepped by hand (`y = 46; do ... y += 35; while (y < 256)`). The two forms compile to the same loop but number temporaries differently, which decides an operand evaluation order 3000 instructions later.
- `if (!f(...))` and `if (f(...) == 0)` compile to the same bytes but differ for the inliner: the negated form is 2 units smaller in own size and about 8 smaller in the 35000 total per use. In a long `else if` chain the spelling per entry therefore moves both the point where the total cap stops inlining and the entry that hosts the merged common tail (the host follows own size before and inside the chain, not expanded callee sizes). `UIGuildMemberManageWnd::OnDraw` needs the negated form on the 20 oldest job entries of each chain and `== 0` on the later ones; all-negated and none both fail.
- A named local that is homed on the stack on our side and has no record in the original PDB was not a user variable there. Two forms make procedures match: writing the value as one expression (`timeGetTime() + rand() % 9 * 1000 + 1500` instead of a named `delay`, `UIWindowMgr::Help_Battle`) and reusing the result variables for the intermediate values (`y = m_curIndex / n; x = m_curIndex - y * n; y = y * 47 + 28; x = x * 47 + 11;`, `UIEmotionListWnd::OnDraw`), which also decides which scratch register carries them. Variables replaced by an induction variable lose their record on both sides, and register-only variables have none, so the absence is evidence only for stack-homed locals.
- A local initialised from a member loses its record when one of its uses spells the member instead: c2 CSEs the member load and copy propagation folds the local into that temporary, whose slot then has no record. `UIEditCtrl3::DrawEditText`: the original lacks a record for `int firstItem = m_scrollOffset;`; writing the `lastItem` bound as `m_scrollOffset + nItem` (the loops keep `firstItem`) gives the original's records exactly.
- Two statements of one expression on original lines with a gap between them were a named local and its use. The local is copy-propagated and leaves no record, but it changes register preferences. `UIEditCtrl3::DrawEditText`: `strlen` on line 2422 and `new` on 2424 came from `int len = strlen(strings[i].c_str()); m_upLineText = new char[len + 1];`, which moves the `c_str` result straight into `edi`. With the member bound above and one `m_x = m_x;` symbol shift, the procedure matches; each part alone leaves 4 to 1272 bytes.
- A caller's stack-homed local without a record can also mean the value lives in a local of an inlined callee: locals of inlined functions get no records, and copy propagation keeps the callee's variable rather than the caller's. The callee's own line table shows the missing statements as lines without records. `UISelCharForUServerWnd::ShowCharInfoBalloon`: the original has no `charInfo` record although the pointer sits in a frame slot, and the standalone `GetCharInfo` has lines 400, 401, 404, 408 and 411 where a single `return (CHARACTER_INFO*)...SendMsg(MM_QUERYCHARICTORINFO, col + row * 6, 0, 0);` after the two divisions has one. With `int index = col + row * 6; CHARACTER_INFO* info = (CHARACTER_INFO*)...SendMsg(MM_QUERYCHARICTORINFO, index, 0, 0); return info;` the standalone bytes stay the same and the caller's inlined copy matches: `index` alone takes the masked diff from 158 lines to 0 (it changes which of `x` and `y` is coloured in the inlined range test and with it `this` and the rotation), `info` alone removes the `charInfo` record and changes no code. `textW` follows `color` in the original's record order (buckets `gy` 11, `gx` 10, `color` 5, then `textW` 10, `nameBuf` 3, `buf` 2), so it is declared in a block that spans the `if`/`else` and the four calls after it; the original lines 435 to 438 and 503 to 504 are free for its braces. The block changes no code.
- An argument that needs a conversion to bind to a reference parameter (`list<UIWindow*>::push_back(wnd)` with a `UIFrameWnd*`) makes a compiler temporary at function scope. Its address is taken, and that changes the alias class of indirections through `this` in the whole function, so loads elsewhere move ahead of stores to inlined arguments. A call through a by-value wrapper (`AddWindow(wnd)`) or a named local in a block has no such effect (`UIWindowMgr::MakeWindow`; "Alias classes of indirections, read from c2").
- A class-type local declared with its initialiser (`iterator it = m.find(k);`) is constructed in place through the hidden return pointer and is a frame local with a record; assigned after a plain declaration it can stay in a register (`UIChatRoomWnd::SendMsg`, `it` at `[ebp+0x18]`).
- Frame slots of scalar locals and temporaries are ordered by reference count, not by declaration. c2 counts one reference per stack-symbol operand of every tuple (`0x10722246`, `AddRef` at `0x1073a9c4`, weight at symbol `+0x34`), keeps the symbols in descending weight (ties: the one that reached the weight first), and places them in that order (`0x10740087`): each goes into the newest existing slot whose members' reference intervals it does not overlap, else into a new slot. When the frame exceeds 0x80 bytes the slots are sorted (quicksort `0x10757404`, not stable) by summed weight × 1000 / slot size, integer division, densest nearest `ebp`. The list is grouped by size, smallest first, and a symbol may join a slot whose size is at least half its own, which grows the slot: a `std::string` can share a slot first opened by a 4-byte scalar. The count precedes the late peephole, so a second use served from a register and an arm removed by tail merging still count.

  A permuted frame with equal instructions therefore means one symbol has one reference more or fewer than the original: `CPc::RenderBodyLayer` needs `switch (layer) { case 2: case 3: renderFlag = RF_NORMAL; break; case 4: renderFlag = RF_NORMAL; break; default: ... }` instead of `if ((layer >= 2 && layer <= 3) || layer == 4)`; both compile to the same compare chain, the switch has one more store before merging (216 differing bytes against 6). Trace the placement at `0x107405b6` (`[[ecx]+0x18]` for the name, `[ecx+0x34]` for the weight); compare visible counts per slot with the original first.
- Before that placement, each parameter's home becomes a pre-slot (`0x107401b3`), heavier parameter first, ties to the one that reached the weight first. A local or temporary first tries the pre-slots in index order (`0x1074025d`) and joins the first whose members' intervals it does not overlap; only then does it get an ordinary slot (`0x107405b6`). An address-taken temporary is live to the end of its scope. Inlined library calls bring their own weighted temporaries, so the call form decides which locals reuse parameter homes. `UIWindowMgr::SendMsg`: `m_children.push_back(w)` expands into two weight-2 temporaries that take the homes of `val1` and `val4`, pushing `ww` to `val3`'s home and growing the frame; `AddWindow(w)`, the form `MakeWindow` uses, gives the original frame.
- String and container locals and compiler temporaries (`operator[]`'s `value_type`, `insert`'s `pair`) of exception-handling functions follow the same rule. In `UIChatRoomWnd::SendMsg` the original's sharing (`memberName` with `wMsg`, `myName` with `strings`) needs `myName` and `memberName` lighter than `wMsg`. Every `return` inside a string's scope is one destructor reference, counted before tail merging, so the number of `return` statements in the scope sets the weight. `int role = -1; if (it != end()) role = map[name]; if (role == -1) return 0;` has one `return` where `if (it == end()) return 0; role = map[name]; if (role == -1) return 0;` has two, and jump threading compiles both to the same code: with the preset form for `myRole` and `memberRole`, `myName` weighs 14 and `memberName` 15 against `wMsg`'s 15, and the frame is the original's. The placement can be traced at `0x10740610` (`esi`) and `0x107406ac` (`[0x1079f224]`), which give symbol, weight, size and the slot joined or created; the per-tuple references at the two `AddRef` call sites `0x107222ab` and `0x1072234d`, with the tuple's line offset (word `[ebx+0x10]`).
- Compare the record order of every unmatched procedure with the order in our `/Z7` object before anything else; a mismatch is a wrong scope or declaration order. Locals declared at function scope that the original declares in a trailing block make the frame larger and stop same-typed locals of sibling blocks from sharing a slot (`UISubChatHisWnd::OnCreate`). In `CGameMode::Zc_Enter_Room` a container stands inside `if (ok) { ... }`, not at function scope after an `if (!ok) return;`; the line table gives the `return` its own line, a conditional jump with a line of its own after the test. Many other corrections change no code; they are still the original's structure.
- Statements that assign constants to plain arrays come before later initialised declarations (`char* names[8] = {...}`) in the stores; when the original stores the assigned values first, the assignments stand above those declarations (`UIEquipWnd::OnCreate`).
- A helper that is inlined shows the statement order its standalone body hides: `GetCharInfo` matches with either order of its two divisions, the copy inside `ShowCharInfoBalloon` only with one.
- An EH state the code never stores and whose unwind action is null is removed dead code that held a `new` expression or a destructible object (decode the map with the FuncInfo behind the handler's `mov eax, offset`). Together with a run of line numbers without code it locates the dead source. Dead code still counts for the inline budget, the state numbers and the codegen entropy of later functions:
  - `UIPartySettingWnd::OnCreate`: two null states, 38 code-free lines; a stand-in `if (0)` with two `new` expressions equalises all but two operand orders and moves a later function's entropy shift from 43 to 42;
  - `CRenderer::AddSpriteIndex`: one null state between the `CacheSurface` local and the `new CTexture`, line 724 code-free; `if (0) m_unusedCacheSurfaces.push_back(new CTexture(...));` there makes it match.

  Such a stand-in is steering: the project counts a statement `if (0)` with no `else` as dead-code steering, in the function and in callers that inline it, so the function counts as identical with markers. An `if (0) ... else ...` is a disabled alternative the authors wrote and does not count.
- Dead stores of locals into a parameter's home slot are locals that c2 homed in memory because removed code read them by value, and whose reads it deleted only afterwards: the stores stay, the locals no longer conflict and pack into the same home. An address-taken local (`if (0) f(v)`) keeps its store too but stays live to the end of its scope and takes another slot. A loop headed by a condition that never holds and that c2 folds late (`while (a < b && a > b) {}`) reproduces it; an empty `if` with the same condition is removed early and loses the stores (`UIEquipWnd::OnMouseHover`: 29 code-free lines inside the `if` whose head the stores precede; the loop there is a stand-in and counts as dead-code steering).
- A stack slot that the original uses and that has no local record in the PDB is a compiler symbol, usually an induction variable c2 made from an expression of a source variable that itself disappeared. `UIBattleMsgOptionWnd::OnDraw`: the 1-based counter slot is `drawCount + 1` of a 0-based `drawCount` (`int viewCount = drawCount + 1;` in the loop body, the break test `drawCount + 1 == m_itemVertNum` as in the sibling list windows of `UIGuildWnd.cpp`); a named 1-based counter gets a record and an extra register copy at its increment. `ExceptionFilter`: the row pointer slot is the closed-form final value of a running index (`g_recentEffectId[index]; index++;` inside `row`/`col` loops); explicit row pointers get a record and the store before the inner counter's initialisation. Look for the idiom in sibling functions first.
- Two block-entry reloads in the reverse order (the candidate-number parity above) flip with one more local referenced early: an initialisation next to the existing ones (`srcVert = NULL;` beside `pTex = 0; pFaceArr = 0;` in `CGrannyPc::RenderAndAni`; any one of three locals makes it match, two restore the old order).
- In the sort keys, the key of a product `i * c` carries the symbol number of `i` (`number << 5` plus a constant), so which operand of a sum becomes the base register depends on when the loop variable's symbol was created, not on the spelling of the sum.
- When call counts differ, read the nested inline walk at the site first: a callee is expanded when its size fits the budget left at its level, callees under 40 are free, and the top-level share is what its nested sites spend. `UIMailListWnd::SendMsg` needs a share of 697 to 802 at one `std::string::assign` site (ours 492); a local for a repeated accessor (fewer sites left) or own-size filler both reach it, and the code then differs only in operand order inside the expanded callee.
- Inside an expanded callee, a sum of a CSE temporary and the callee's parameter symbol is ordered by `temporary number << 6` against `symbol number << 5` (both taken modulo 65536). Such a tie does not move with the codegen-entropy count or with one or two accessors; it needs the temporary count before that point to change by hundreds, which no steering macro does.
- Pair the original's line records with ours by code offset even when the procedure is not identical: a run of statements whose line numbers differ from ours by one constant has the same statement layout, and a code-bearing line where we have none is a statement we fold into another. `CGrannyCorpse3d::Render`: `fmul -40; fchs; fadd m_pos.x` (negation not folded into the constant) with its own line only comes from the product as a separate statement (`float c1 = cosDeg * -40.0f;` ... `GetHeight(m_pos.x + (0.0f * cosDeg - s1), ...)`); every one-expression spelling folds the sign.
- `fst` after a call that returns a float (value kept on the x87 stack and used at once) against our `fstp`/`fld` means the two calls are in the other order: the value used first is computed last.
- Float locals stay on the x87 stack only while their uses fit the stack order, and named float results compete with them for it. Measured in a standalone reproducer: of two locals `tu`, `tv` defined in that order and used twice each, both stay on the stack when the first use is of `tv` (use orders v u v u and v v u u), and `tv` is stored to a home when `tu` is used first (u u v v, u v u v); when the four results are named floats, they take the stack places and `tu`/`tv` get homes and local records. Values that sit in frame slots without records while the inputs stay on the stack are therefore not named floats: they are members of a class-type local.

  `C3dDevice::TestScreen`: the original keeps `tu` and `tv` on the stack, stores three of the four texture coordinates in unrecorded slots and keeps the fourth on the stack. `float u0 = tu / float(test.m_w);` and its three siblings give 153 to 225 differing lines in every order and conversion form. `vector2d uv[2] = { vector2d(tu / test.m_w, tv / test.m_h), vector2d((tu + 4) / test.m_w, (tv + 4) / test.m_h) };`, the statement `CTexture::DrawSurfaceStretch` uses, gives the original's code: the array's members are packed as separate slots, the store of `uv[1].x` is forwarded, and the array leaves no record here (the same statement in the standalone reproducer keeps one). The shape to recognise is the one `DrawSurfaceStretch` shows: the first divisor's `fild qword` stays on the stack (`fld; fdiv st, st(1)`), the second is stored as a float and divided from memory, and `fstp st(0)` drops the first after the third value. Look for the statement in a function that already matches before trying forms of one's own.
- Two locals of one scope whose names are exchanged compile to the same bytes; the record's slot says which is which. `TestScreen`: the sum taken under `if (dwBuffer[k])` is `white` (`[ebp-0x1c]`), the other `black`, and `int white = 0;` comes first (the other order swaps the two zero stores). With `srcDesc`, `j` and `byteCount` declared in the `while` body and the final `x`/`y` written into the `m_uOffset`/`m_vOffset` expressions, records and frame slots equal the original's.
- Local symbols are numbered at their first reference in the function body, not at their declaration (`int destx;` numbered where it is first assigned; `int destx = 0;` numbered there). Two consequences:
  - `a + b` of two locals puts the higher number first, so `w + destx > 90` computed in `w`'s register (`add edx, eax`) means `destx` was referenced before `w`: an initialiser on its declaration (`int destx = 0;`, a dead store that leaves no code) is enough.
  - A load through a pointer local at offset 0 (`clip->x`, op 0x14c, key `0001x007`) sorts by `0x10007 | (pointer's number & 7) << 13`, against `0x10000 | number << 5` for a plain local. Its place in a sum therefore depends on the count of locals referenced before the pointer, modulo 8, and the declaration-count entropy does not move it. `UIPetInfoWnd::OnDraw` needs 6 or 7 more: a separate `CBitmapRes*` local for each bitmap instead of one reused variable is code-neutral and adds one each. Register-only locals have no PDB record, so their number is free; stack locals and scopes stay bound by the record order. A local for a repeated subexpression (`m_w - 24`) is not code-neutral.
- The symbols an inline expansion makes (its `this`, parameters, locals) are numbered in a run of their own: adding or removing the caller's locals does not move them, another expansion earlier in the function does. `std::string::assign`'s terminator store `_Ptr[_N] = 0` (`mov byte ptr [eax + ecx], 0` against `[ecx + eax]`) is ordered by `0x10007 | (number of the expansion's pointer symbol & 7) << 13` against the `_N` symbol's key, so a function with several such stores that are all swapped, or swapped at every second site, is off by a fixed count of expansion symbols modulo 8 (`UISelCharForUServerWnd::OnCreate`: +2).
- Working a symbol-number case: each operand of the sum is either a load through a pointer local at offset 0 (op 0x14c, key `0001x007`, base N, where N is the local's number when the pointer is a local and a temporary's when it is a CSE temporary, whose key does not move) or a symbol (op 0x14a, symbol N); the operands are emitted highest key first. Read which class the pointer needs, then add or remove register-only locals referenced before it. Code-neutral ones found so far: a separate variable instead of a reused one (`CActRes* actRes` per loop, one `CBitmapRes*` per bitmap), a `const char*` local for a `c_str()` argument, `int x = 0;` on a declaration. Not neutral: a local for the object of a chained call (`((CActRes*)Get(...))->GetMotion(a, b)` pushes `a, b` before `Get`; with a local `Get` runs first), a local for a repeated subexpression. `UIMakeCharWnd::OnDraw`: +3 before the second loop's `clip`.
- Line records locate a wrong statement shape before any byte work: merge the original's and our records by code offset. A source line of ours whose record the original lacks is not a separate statement there; an original record with no line of ours is a statement we do not have. `CGrannyCorpse3d::Render` and `C3dGrannyGameActor::RenderNormal` each have a record between the index load and `ProjectVertex`: a pointer local (`granny_pnt332_vertex* src = &srcVert[vertNo];`, then `src->Position[0]`...). Indexing `srcVert[vertNo]` at every use gives equal-looking code with the index kept in another register and `this` not spilled.
- Which instructions start a record is evidence even when the line numbers are not (blank lines and brace style shift them). Align the original's instructions with ours and list the places where only one side starts a record. Readings, all from `UISelectCharWnd::OnDraw` and its matched siblings:
  - The jump over an `else` part carries the line of the `else`. A jump and the test after it under one record are `else if (...)`; with `continue;` or a separate `if` the test has a record of its own. This one changes the code: see the predecessor order under "The split of candidates left without a register".
  - A multi-line statement or condition carries its last line, so `if (a && b &&` / `c) {` is one record and `if (a && b) {` / `if (c) {` two. Code-neutral where measured.
  - `offX += e;` on a variable known to be zero is folded into the statement before it and has no record; `offX = e;` has one. `curMotion = accMotion = f();` has one record, the two assignments two; `int offX = 0, offY = 0;` one. All three code-neutral where measured.
  - A statement and the increment after it under two records, the second on a bare `inc reg`, are two statements on the variable the register holds. `UINoticeWnd::OnCreate`: `fwrite(fileBuf1, 1, 1, file2); fileBuf1++;` in the copy loop (records on the call and on `inc esi`), not `fwrite(fileBuf1 + i, ...)`; `lineStart[count++] = i + 1;` has one record, the store and a separate `count++;` two.
- Code-free line runs are evidence too. Pair the original's records with ours and look for each step where the original advances more lines than we do. Brace style accounts for one line per `{` and two per `else`. A run at the end of a branch, before its `}`, can be the branch's own copy of the statements the other branch ends with: tail merging runs after register allocation, keeps the later copy and its line numbers, and leaves the earlier copy's lines without code. The copies are separate tuples when the candidates are weighed, so they change keys and picks without changing a byte of the merged code.

  `UINoticeWnd::OnCreate`: the message loop's `Move` and `AddChild` stand in both branches of `if (j < count)` (original lines 1498/1499 code-free, 1508/1509 kept). With one copy after the `if`, `this` (key 228) is picked after the button pointer (244), the zero constant is cut at the button loop and `count` loses its register from its definition to the parse loop (350 differing lines); the second copy references `this` and the row offset in the true branch, which reorders both picks. A dead read of a member at the same place (`while (m < 0 && m > 0) {}`, swept over every statement boundary) locates such a block before the natural form is known.
- Inside c2 a tuple's line is the 16-bit field at `+0x10`, relative to the function's first line. New tuples take the global current line `[0x107ac360]`, which every walker sets from the tuple it visits; the split sets it from the reference tuple (`0x1073db2e`, `0x1073dc3d`), the block-end writer `0x107102bd` from the block's last tuple, and the jump an edge split adds (`0x1073a764`) takes the line of its block's last tuple (`0x1073a877`). The scheduler appends through `0x10727e6a`, which raises a tuple's line to that of the tuple before it, so records never decrease inside a scheduled region and a reload placed before a label passes its line to the label and the block after it. A record that seems out of place (a loop's `for` line on a reload in the middle of the body) is such a stamp, not a statement.
- `vector3d::Set(a, b, c)` and three member assignments differ: with `Set`, a later inline `Normalize()` multiplies `fld x; fmul st, st(1)`; with the assignments, `fld st(0); fmul x`.
- The numbers of a module's `$E` and `$S` helpers are evidence of its leading declarations: c1 numbers them from a counter that some declarations ahead of them advance (`GuildInfo.h` by 6, the ATL headers by 3, `typedef int` lines by none), and the original PDB keeps the names in each module's symbols. Compare them with `llvm-nm` of our object. Modules measured with numbers other than the original's: `LoginMode.cpp` (`$E78` against our `$E76`), `CReAssemblyPacket.cpp` and `CSRCryptoR2PacketConfiguration.cpp` (`$E21` against `$E19`), `UIControl2.cpp` (`$E69` against `$E66`), `window/UISys.cpp` (`$E64` against `$E51`), `LanguageKeyProcess.cpp` (`$E36` against `$E19`), `PathFinder.cpp` (`$E68` against `$E27`), `3dlib/Renderer.cpp` in its last pair only (`$E112` against `$E108`), and `GameMode.cpp`, where the original has two more names (`$S116`, `$S117`).

## Size limits inside c2

- Inline walk (0x10731dd1): a callee's size is read as a signed 16-bit word, the caller's budget is clamp(2 x own size, 1000, 35000), and callees of size <= 0x28 are free. An own size of 32768 or more wraps negative and leaves the floor budget of 1000.
- Global optimizer (0x10732ce1): a function with more than 10000 tuples is compiled in a degraded mode (`0x1079d6b4 = 1`): no per-candidate kill analysis, stack temporaries no longer share slots, visibly worse code throughout. A function whose frame grows by 4 bytes per temporary and whose instruction count jumps after adding code has crossed this limit. A breakpoint at `0x10732dd7` shows the count in `ebp`.
- Alias classes (0x10751759): pointer-derived classes numbered 0x400 or higher become class 1, which aliases everything ("Alias classes of indirections, read from c2").
- Frame packer (0x10740087): when slot joins exist and the frame is 0x70 or larger it discards the pre-assigned slots and places every non-parameter symbol again; frames above 0x80 are sorted (0x10757404).
